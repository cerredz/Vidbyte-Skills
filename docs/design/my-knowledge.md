**Status:** Draft

## 1. Overview

The `my-knowledge` skill is an explicit-invocation metacognitive probe that assesses the user's genuine understanding of topics covered in a session. On invocation via `/my-knowledge`, the model retrospectively scans the full conversation, observes behavioral signals (tells) that discriminate genuine from surface understanding, synthesizes a three-tier knowledge map, presents a targeted cue-only probe of 2-3 questions, and delivers an honest report showing the gap between what the user *feels* they know and what they can likely *reproduce independently*.

The skill is grounded in Asher Koriat's cue-utilization framework (1997) and the foresight-bias findings of Koriat & Bjork (2005): when people study with the answer present, they systematically overestimate their understanding because the feeling of fluency is generated by the richness of current context, not by the robustness of independent recall. An AI-assisted working session is the worst possible environment for accurate self-assessment — the model has explained everything, the code is visible, the conversation history contains every answer. `/my-knowledge` corrects for this by providing an honest, external read on where understanding is solid, surface, or missing — while the session is still open and the user can act on it.

---

## 2. Goals & Non-Goals

### Goals
- Create a single `SKILL.md` that the LLM agent reads and follows as procedural instructions
- Scan the full conversation history at invocation time and identify all distinct topics covered
- Detect behavioral signals (tells) that discriminate genuine independent understanding from context-dependent surface familiarity
- Synthesize a three-tier knowledge map: **solid** (independently demonstrated), **surface** (exposed but not independently demonstrated), and **gap** (discussed but no evidence of retention)
- Generate 2-3 cue-only probe questions — questions that strip away the rich context present during the session and test whether the user can generate understanding from memory
- Deliver an honest, evidence-backed report that makes the solid/surface/gap distinction explicit
- Operate entirely as an inline response — no files written, no CLI calls, no backend

### Non-Goals
- Running silently in the background (unlike `why` or `anti-passive`) — this is an explicit-invocation skill
- Persisting state across sessions — each `/my-knowledge` invocation assesses the current session only
- Evaluating the user's answers to the cue-only probe — the skill presents questions and stops; the user decides whether to answer
- Replacing any part of the normal session flow — the skill is purely a reporting tool
- Installing any new runtime code or modifying the installer
- Modifying validation scripts — the skill passes existing validation unchanged
- Acting as a tutor or providing corrective instruction — it assesses, it does not teach
- Making the user feel judged or evaluated — tone must be curious, observational, and collaborative

## 3. Background & Context

### Research Foundation

Asher Koriat's 1997 paper "Monitoring One's Own Knowledge During Study: A Cue-Utilization Approach to Judgments of Learning" (J. Exp. Psych.: General, 126(4), 349-370) established that when people study material with the answer present, they consistently and systematically overestimate how well they will later recall it. This is called Judgment of Learning (JOL) inflation, and it happens because the feeling of understanding is driven by the richness of the current context, not by the robustness of what the learner could independently reproduce.

Koriat identifies three types of cues people use to judge their own learning:

| Cue Type | What It Is | Why It Misleads |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Intrinsic** | Perceived difficulty or relatedness of the material itself | Items feel easy when both the question and answer are visible together — that ease is not predictive of later recall from the question alone |
| **Extrinsic** | Circumstances of study (how many times seen, how much time spent) | Repeated exposure to explained material feels like mastery; it is often mere familiarity |
| **Mnemonic** | Internal fluency signal — how easily the concept "flows" right now | Fluency is highest when context is richest. In an AI session, context is maximally rich. Fluency is therefore maximally misleading |

Koriat & Bjork (2005) "Illusions of Competence in Monitoring One's Knowledge During Study" introduced the concept of **foresight bias**: making predictions about future recall in the presence of the answer produces inflated confidence. The only reliable fix is to use **cue-only probes** — presenting the question or concept without the answer or supporting context and measuring what the learner can generate independently.

### Why This Matters for an AI Coding/Learning Session

A working session with an AI assistant is the worst possible environment for accurate self-assessment:

- The AI has explained everything
- The code or output is right there on the screen
- The conversation history contains every answer
- The user can re-read any explanation in seconds

This means the user's felt sense of understanding during a session is systematically inflated relative to their actual independent understanding. They leave the session feeling solid — and then hit a wall the next day when the context is gone and they have to apply it from memory or first principles.

`/my-knowledge` exists to correct for this. It does not replace the AI assistant or make the session adversarial. It gives the user an honest, external read on where their understanding is genuinely solid and where it is context-dependent — while the session is still open and they can do something about it.

### Relationship to Existing Skills

| Skill | Function | Overlap / Distinction |
|-------|----------|----------------------|
| `why` | Probes metacognitive depth on decisions | `why` probes decision-making in real time; `my-knowledge` assesses knowledge retention retrospectively |
| `anti-passive` | Detects and interrupts passive consumption | `anti-passive` monitors activity level; `my-knowledge` assesses understanding depth on demand |
| `misconceptions` | Tracks and surfaces wrong beliefs | `misconceptions` looks for incorrect mental models; `my-knowledge` distinguishes known from context-dependent |
| `concept-coverage` | Tracks which concepts have been covered | `concept-coverage` maps topics; `my-knowledge` assesses depth within those topics |
| `blindspots` | Identifies knowledge gaps from user prompts | `blindspots` finds gaps reactively; `my-knowledge` actively probes and tiers knowledge |
| `transfer-signals` | Tracks cross-field concept application | `transfer-signals` watches for transfer; `my-knowledge` tells the user what transferred vs. didn't |
| `daily-review` | Generates end-of-session review | `daily-review` catalogs what was done; `my-knowledge` assesses what was learned |

None of these existing skills does what `/my-knowledge` does: provide a structured, evidence-based assessment of the user's current knowledge state grounded in the JOL research framework, complete with a cue-only probe that strips away context to reveal the gap between felt and actual understanding.

## 4. Requirements

### Functional Requirements

1. The skill SHALL activate only on explicit user invocation via the `/my-knowledge` slash command.
2. On invocation, the skill SHALL scan the full conversation history (user prompts and agent responses) to identify all distinct topics discussed during the session.
3. The skill SHALL identify topics at the level of named concepts, techniques, frameworks, patterns, technologies, or processes — not at the level of individual facts or single exchanges.
4. The skill SHALL, for each identified topic, evaluate the conversation history for behavioral signals (tells) that indicate the user's depth of understanding. These tells SHALL be drawn from the defined taxonomy in Requirement 5.
5. The skill SHALL use the following behavioral signal taxonomy to classify understanding depth:

   **Signals of Solid Understanding (the user has demonstrated independent command):**
   - **Independent generation**: The user wrote, built, or produced something using the concept without being given a complete solution first
   - **Contextual application**: The user applied the concept to a new problem or context that was not explicitly demonstrated earlier in the session
   - **Self-correction**: The user identified their own error related to the concept and corrected it without being told
   - **Conceptual explanation**: The user explained the concept back in their own words, using original examples, not paraphrasing the agent
   - **Structural questioning**: The user asked questions about how the concept connects to other concepts, not just what it is
   - **Challenge or refinement**: The user questioned, refined, or extended a suggestion from the agent rather than accepting it wholesale
   - **Deliberate deviation**: The user consciously chose a different approach than what the agent suggested and articulated why

   **Signals of Surface / Context-Dependent Understanding (the user can follow along but hasn't demonstrated independent command):**
   - **Passive agreement**: The user said "makes sense", "got it", "I understand" after an explanation, but never demonstrated the understanding independently
   - **Copy-paste consumption**: The user used provided code or solutions without modification, adaptation, or extension
   - **Topic re-asking**: The user asked essentially the same question about the same topic later in the session (indicating the earlier explanation didn't stick)
   - **Definition-chasing**: The user's follow-up questions were about clarifying terminology or surface details rather than deeper application or connection
   - **Dependency-seeking**: The user asked "how do I do X?" for every X without attempting anything independently first
   - **Narrowing questions**: The user's questions about a topic got progressively narrower (more specific trivia) rather than broader (connecting to other concepts or applications)
   - **Unearned confidence**: The user expressed confidence about understanding ("I know how X works now") without any evidence of independent recall or application

   **Signals of Knowledge Gap (the topic was discussed but the user shows no evidence of engaging with it):**
   - **No interaction**: The topic appeared in an agent response but the user never engaged with it
   - **Topic avoidance**: The user explicitly deferred or ignored a topic ("we'll come back to that", "I don't need to know that right now")
   - **Single-mention drift**: The topic was mentioned once and the user immediately changed the subject
   - **Unrecognized prerequisite**: The user discussed a topic that depends on a prerequisite concept they showed no evidence of understanding

6. The skill SHALL assign each topic to one of three tiers:

   - **Solid**: At least 2 independent generation or application signals, and no contradictory surface signals
   - **Surface**: At least 1 surface signal, and fewer than 2 independent generation/application signals
   - **Gap**: The topic was discussed but no signals of engagement (solid or surface) are present

7. The skill SHALL generate a cue-only probe of 2-3 questions that test the user's independent understanding by stripping away the rich context that was present during the session. Each probe question SHALL:
   - Remove all scaffolding, hints, code, and explanations that were present when the topic was originally discussed
   - Require generation, not recognition (e.g., "What are the three steps to..." not "Which of these is correct...")
   - Reference a specific topic from the session without providing any of the information that was available during the session
   - Include at least one question targeting a topic classified as Solid (to validate the assessment method) and at least one targeting a topic classified as Surface (to reveal the JOL inflation)
   - Use direct, concrete language: "How would you implement X?" or "What happens when Y?" or "Why does Z cause W?"

8. The skill SHALL deliver its assessment as a structured inline report with the following sections, in order:

   **Section 1 — Session Summary**: A brief (2-3 sentence) overview of what the session covered, listing the main topic areas.

   **Section 2 — Solid Knowledge**: Each topic the user has demonstrated solid independent understanding of, with:
   - The topic name
   - A brief description of what was covered
   - The specific evidence (tells) that support the Solid classification
   - A note on transfer potential: where this knowledge could apply beyond the current context

   **Section 3 — Surface Knowledge**: Each topic where the user has exposure but not independent command, with:
   - The topic name
   - A brief description of what was covered
   - The specific surface signals observed
   - Why the topic may feel solid despite being context-dependent (referencing the relevant cue type: intrinsic, extrinsic, or mnemonic)
   - A specific recommendation for what practice would move this topic to Solid

   **Section 4 — Knowledge Gaps**: Topics discussed but not retained, with:
   - The topic name
   - Why it matters (connection to the user's stated goals)
   - A suggested entry point for independent exploration

   **Section 5 — Cue-Only Probe**: 2-3 questions formatted as a self-test, with:
   - Clear instructions: "Answer these from memory without scrolling up or searching. The goal is to find out what you can generate independently, not what you can recognize."
   - Each question on its own line, numbered
   - No answers provided (the skill assesses, it does not teach)

   **Section 6 — Session Honesty Index**: A single-sentence summary that explicitly names the gap between felt and actual understanding, e.g., "Of the [N] topics discussed this session, [X] are at a level where you could apply them independently tomorrow; [Y] feel solid right now but are likely context-dependent."

9. The report SHALL be evidence-based. Every classification (Solid, Surface, Gap) SHALL reference at least one specific observed behavior from the conversation. The report SHALL NOT rely on the model's general assessment of difficulty or importance.

10. The report SHALL use a supportive, curious, non-judgmental tone. The skill is an honest mirror, not a critic. The implicit message is "here's what the evidence shows — you get to decide what to do with it."

11. The skill SHALL NOT write any files to disk. The report is delivered entirely inline.

12. The skill SHALL NOT call any CLI commands, external services, or Vidbyte endpoints. This is a pure prompt skill.

13. The skill SHALL handle edge cases gracefully:
    - **Very short session (< 5 exchanges)**: Report should note limited evidence and offer a lighter assessment
    - **Single-topic session**: Report should still provide tiered depth assessment within that single topic
    - **No solid signals found**: Report should acknowledge this honestly without judgment and focus the cue-only probe on potential surface areas
    - **Session is pure chat (no technical/learning content)**: Report should note that the session was conversational, not instructional, and that knowledge assessment is not applicable

### Non-Functional Requirements

- **Performance**: The skill processes the conversation history once at invocation time. For sessions under 200 exchanges, the scan and synthesis should complete within a single response turn.
- **Scalability**: No persistent state. Each invocation is independent.
- **Security**: No file writes, no network calls, no credential exposure.
- **Observability**: The report itself is the only output.
- **Reliability**: When behavioral signals are ambiguous, the skill SHALL default to Surface classification. Overestimating understanding (calling something Solid when it's Surface) is worse than underestimating it (calling something Surface when it might be Solid).

## 5. High-Level Design

The skill is a single `SKILL.md` file that functions as a procedural instruction set for the LLM agent. The agent reads these instructions when the user invokes `/my-knowledge` and executes a four-phase assessment pipeline.

**Data flow:**

```
User invokes /my-knowledge
         |
         v
[Agent loads my-knowledge SKILL.md]
         |
         +-- Phase 1: Session Scan
         |     - Read full conversation history
         |     - Extract distinct topics discussed
         |     - Collect all user prompts and agent responses per topic
         |
         v
[Phase 2: Signal Detection & Tiering]
         |     - For each topic, evaluate user messages against
         |       the behavioral signal taxonomy
         |     - Count solid signals and surface signals per topic
         |     - Classify each topic as Solid, Surface, or Gap
         |
         v
[Phase 3: Cue-Only Probe Generation]
         |     - Select 1 topic from Solid tier (to validate)
         |     - Select 1-2 topics from Surface tier (to reveal JOL inflation)
         |     - Generate questions that strip away context
         |     - Format as a self-test block
         |
         v
[Phase 4: Report Assembly]
               - Build Section 1 (Session Summary)
               - Build Section 2 (Solid Knowledge)
               - Build Section 3 (Surface Knowledge)
               - Build Section 4 (Knowledge Gaps)
               - Build Section 5 (Cue-Only Probe)
               - Build Section 6 (Session Honesty Index)
               - Deliver as inline response
```

**Key design decisions:**

1. **Explicit invocation only, not background**: Unlike `why` and `anti-passive` which run silently, `my-knowledge` is a heavy analytical operation that the user explicitly requests. Running it in the background on every prompt would be wasteful and intrusive. The slash-command pattern matches how users think about assessment — you check your knowledge at a stopping point, not continuously.

2. **Evidence-based tiering, not model-judgment tiering**: The skill does not ask the agent "how well does the user understand X?" — a question that would produce unreliable, model-biased answers. Instead, it asks "which specific behavioral signals did the user exhibit for X?" — an observable, evidence-based question. The tier assignment is algorithmic (count signals, apply thresholds) rather than subjective.

3. **Cue-only probe as the centerpiece**: The probe is not a quiz. It is the practical application of Koriat's core finding: the only way to get an accurate read on understanding is to strip away context and ask the user to generate. The probe questions are designed to create the moment of "oh, I thought I knew this but I can't actually explain it" — which is the exact correction for JOL inflation.

4. **Three-tier, not binary**: A binary "know / don't know" classification would miss the most important category — the surface understanding that feels like mastery. The Surface tier is the skill's primary contribution: surfacing what is context-dependent so the user can make it context-independent.

5. **No teaching, only assessing**: The skill does not provide answers to the probe questions, explanations of concepts, or tutorials. It assesses and reports. This keeps the skill focused and avoids the conflict of interest where the assessor also becomes the tutor.

6. **Prompt-only architecture**: Same rationale as `why` and `anti-passive` — universal portability, no runtime dependencies, works in any harness. No files, no CLI, no backend.

## 6. Detailed Design

### 6.1 SKILL.md (Skill Definition)

**File(s):** `skills/my-knowledge/SKILL.md`
**Type:** New file

#### What it does
The complete skill definition. Contains YAML frontmatter for discovery/installation, plus the full procedural instructions the LLM agent follows when the user invokes `/my-knowledge` to assess their session understanding.

#### Interface / API

Frontmatter:
```yaml
---
name: my-knowledge
description: >
  Use when the user invokes /my-knowledge to get an honest assessment
  of what they genuinely understand versus what feels solid but is
  context-dependent. Scans the session, detects behavioral signals,
  tiers knowledge into Solid/Surface/Gap, and delivers a cue-only probe.
---
```

Body sections:
1. **Identity** — You are an honest knowledge assessor grounded in the JOL research framework. You observe behavioral signals, not felt confidence. You tier knowledge into solid, surface, and gap. You present cue-only probes that strip away context to reveal what the user can generate independently.
2. **Goal** — Give the user an accurate, evidence-based map of what they genuinely understand versus what feels solid but is context-dependent, so they can act on it while the session is still open.
3. **Research Foundation** — The Koriat cue-utilization framework: intrinsic, extrinsic, and mnemonic cues; foresight bias; why AI sessions maximize JOL inflation.
4. **Phase 1 — Session Scan** — Instructions for extracting topics, collecting evidence per topic.
5. **Phase 2 — Signal Detection & Tiering** — The behavioral signal taxonomy (solid signals, surface signals, gap signals), the tier assignment algorithm, and a note on conservative classification.
6. **Phase 3 — Cue-Only Probe Generation** — How to select topics for probing, how to strip context from questions, how to formulate generation-requiring questions.
7. **Phase 4 — Report Assembly** — The six-section output structure with formatting, tone guidance, and section-by-section instructions.
8. **Edge Cases** — Very short sessions, single-topic sessions, no-solid-signals sessions, pure-chat sessions.
9. **Constraints** — Guardrails (no files, no CLI, no teaching, no judgment, evidence-only classifications).
10. **Tone** — Curious, observational, evidence-backed, collaborative. The skill is a mirror, not a judge.
11. **Success Criteria** — Verifiable outcomes.
12. **Input** — Invocation behavior (explicit `/my-knowledge` only).

#### Logic / Algorithm

**Phase 1 — Session Scan:**

1. Read the full conversation history from session start to the current message.
2. Identify all distinct topics discussed. A topic is a named concept, technique, framework, pattern, technology, or process that was the subject of at least one substantive exchange (not just a passing mention).
3. For each topic, collect all user prompts and relevant agent responses that relate to that topic.
4. Group related sub-topics under broader topic headings (e.g., "PostgreSQL indexing" and "composite keys" both fall under "Database schema design").
5. Target 3-8 topics for reporting. Sessions with more topics should consolidate; sessions with fewer should respect the actual count.

**Phase 2 — Signal Detection & Tiering:**

For each identified topic, scan the collected user messages for the behavioral signals defined in the taxonomy.

**Solid signals** (evidence of independent command):
- **Independent generation**: User wrote/built/produced using the concept without being given a complete solution
- **Contextual application**: User applied the concept to a new problem/context not explicitly demonstrated
- **Self-correction**: User identified their own error related to the concept
- **Conceptual explanation**: User explained the concept in their own words with original examples
- **Structural questioning**: User asked about how the concept connects to other concepts
- **Challenge or refinement**: User questioned, refined, or extended an agent suggestion
- **Deliberate deviation**: User chose a different approach and articulated why

**Surface signals** (evidence of context-dependent familiarity):
- **Passive agreement**: User agreed ("makes sense", "got it") without independent demonstration
- **Copy-paste consumption**: User used provided code/solutions without modification
- **Topic re-asking**: User asked essentially the same question about the topic later
- **Definition-chasing**: Follow-up questions focused on terminology/surface details
- **Dependency-seeking**: User asked "how do I do X?" for every X without attempting independently
- **Narrowing questions**: Questions got progressively narrower rather than broader
- **Unearned confidence**: User expressed understanding confidence without evidence of recall/application

**Gap signals** (evidence of non-engagement):
- **No interaction**: Topic appeared in an agent response but user never engaged
- **Topic avoidance**: User explicitly deferred or ignored
- **Single-mention drift**: Topic mentioned once, user immediately changed subject
- **Unrecognized prerequisite**: User discussed topic that depends on prerequisite they don't show

**Tier assignment algorithm:**

For each topic:
1. Count solid signals. Count surface signals. Count gap signals.
2. If solid signals >= 2 and no contradictory surface signals that suggest the generation was scaffolded: **Solid**
3. If surface signals >= 1 and solid signals < 2: **Surface**
4. If gap signals are the only signals present: **Gap**
5. If the evidence is ambiguous or mixed (e.g., 1 solid signal + 2 surface signals), apply conservative classification: **Surface**. Only classify as Solid when the evidence is clear and uncontradicted.
6. If no signals of any type are detected (topic was purely in agent responses, user never engaged): classify as **Gap** with the note "topic discussed but you did not engage with it."

**Phase 3 — Cue-Only Probe Generation:**

1. Select probe targets:
   - 1 topic from the Solid tier (to validate — if the user can answer, the assessment is accurate)
   - 1-2 topics from the Surface tier (to reveal JOL inflation — these are the topics that feel solid but aren't)

2. For each selected topic, formulate a question that:
   - **Strips away context**: Removes all hints, code examples, explanations, and scaffolding that were present when the topic was discussed
   - **Requires generation**: Asks the user to produce something (explain, implement, predict, compare) rather than recognize something (identify, select, confirm)
   - **References the specific topic** without providing any information about it: "In your own words, what are the three steps to..." not "The three steps we discussed were X, Y, Z — can you list them?"
   - **Uses direct, concrete language**: "How would you..." "What happens when..." "Why does..." "Write a function that..."

3. Format the probe as a self-test block:
   ```text
   ## Cue-Only Probe — Self-Test

   Answer these from memory. Don't scroll up. Don't search. The goal is to find out what you can generate independently, not what you can recognize.

   1. [Question targeting a Solid topic]
   2. [Question targeting a Surface topic]
   3. [Optional question targeting a second Surface topic]
   ```

4. Quality checks before finalizing:
   - Each question is answerable from the session content
   - Each question strips away the context that was present during learning
   - No question can be answered by simple recognition or paraphrasing
   - The Solid-topic question is not a "gotcha" — it should be answerable if the assessment is correct

**Phase 4 — Report Assembly:**

Build the six-section report in order. Every classification must reference at least one specific observed behavior.

**Section 1 — Session Summary:**
- 2-3 sentences covering: what the session was about, how many distinct topic areas were identified, the overall knowledge profile (e.g., "X solid, Y surface, Z gaps")
- Do not list every topic here — save detail for subsequent sections

**Section 2 — Solid Knowledge:**
- Only include topics classified as Solid (2+ independent generation/application signals)
- If no topics meet the threshold, include the heading and state: "No topics met the threshold for solid independent understanding in this session. This is not unusual — see the Surface section below for topics that may feel solid but are likely context-dependent."
- For each solid topic, include:
  - Topic name (bold)
  - What was covered (1-2 sentences)
  - Evidence: at least one specific behavioral signal observed, quoted or referenced from the conversation
  - Transfer potential: where this knowledge could be applied beyond the current context

**Section 3 — Surface Knowledge:**
- Include all topics classified as Surface
- For each surface topic, include:
  - Topic name (bold)
  - What was covered (1-2 sentences)
  - Evidence: the specific surface signals observed
  - Why it may feel solid: identify which cue type(s) are likely inflating the JOL (intrinsic — the answer was visible alongside the question; extrinsic — the topic was discussed at length; mnemonic — the explanation was fluent and felt easy to follow)
  - Recommendation: specific practice that would move this to Solid (e.g., "Try implementing X from scratch without referencing the code we wrote")

**Section 4 — Knowledge Gaps:**
- Include all topics classified as Gap
- For each gap topic, include:
  - Topic name (bold)
  - Why it matters: connection to the user's stated goals or the session's purpose
  - Suggested entry point: a concrete first step for independent exploration
- If no gaps exist, state: "All discussed topics show at least surface-level engagement."

**Section 5 — Cue-Only Probe:**
- The formatted self-test block as designed in Phase 3

**Section 6 — Session Honesty Index:**
- A single paragraph that:
  - Names the numbers: "Of the [N] distinct topics discussed, [X] are solid, [Y] are surface, [Z] are gaps."
  - Names the gap: "The biggest gap between felt and actual understanding appears to be [specific topic or pattern]."
  - Offers a forward-looking note: "The Surface topics are the most actionable — each has a concrete practice that would move it to Solid before the session ends."
- Do not moralize, judge, or prescribe. The tone is observational: "here's what the evidence shows."

#### Edge Cases & Error Handling

- **Very short session (< 5 exchanges)**: The evidence base is thin. The report should note this explicitly: "This session has limited interaction to assess from. The assessment below is based on [N] exchanges and should be treated as provisional." Still perform the scan — even short sessions can reveal surface patterns — but flag the uncertainty.
- **Single-topic session**: All exchanges relate to one topic. Provide a depth assessment within that single topic: which aspects of the topic are Solid, which are Surface, which are Gaps. The tiering applies to sub-domains of the single topic.
- **No solid signals found**: Honest acknowledgment without judgment. The report should say: "No topics met the threshold for solid independent understanding. This is common in sessions focused on exploration, explanation, or decision-making rather than implementation." Focus the cue-only probe on the strongest Surface candidates.
- **Session is pure chat (no technical/learning content)**: The conversation is social, logistical, or purely conversational. The report should say: "This session appears to be conversational rather than instructional. A knowledge assessment is not applicable — there were no distinct topics with enough technical depth to tier." Do not fabricate topics.
- **Very long session (100+ exchanges)**: Topic consolidation is essential. Group related sub-topics under broader headings. Target 5-8 topics maximum for reporting, regardless of actual topic count. Note that some granularity is lost due to session length.
- **User invokes `/my-knowledge` at the very start of a session**: There is no history to scan. Respond: "No conversation history to assess yet. Invoke /my-knowledge after you've covered some ground — the assessment needs at least a few exchanges to be meaningful."
- **Ambiguous signal classification**: When a behavior could be interpreted as either solid or surface (e.g., user writes code but it closely follows the agent's template), default to Surface. Conservative classification avoids the more costly error of calling something Solid when it isn't.

## 10. Testing Plan

### Unit Tests
N/A — There is no executable code to unit test. The skill is a Markdown prompt.

### Integration Tests
N/A — The skill operates within the LLM's session context and produces only inline text output.

### Validation Tests
- **`npm test`** must pass — the `validate.js` script checks that:
  - `skills/my-knowledge/SKILL.md` exists
  - Frontmatter has valid `name: my-knowledge` matching the directory
  - Frontmatter has non-empty `description`
  - Body is non-empty
  - Skill name matches `^[a-z0-9]+(-[a-z0-9]+)*$` regex (`my-knowledge` validates: two lowercase segments separated by a hyphen)

### Manual / QA Test Cases

1. **Basic invocation**: Given a session with 15-20 exchanges covering 4 distinct technical topics with mixed engagement, when the user invokes `/my-knowledge`, then the response contains all six report sections with at least one topic in each tier.

2. **Solid classification accuracy**: Given a session where the user independently wrote a function, debugged it, and explained the approach in their own words, when assessed, then that topic is classified as Solid with specific behavioral evidence cited.

3. **Surface classification accuracy**: Given a session where the user said "makes sense" and "got it" after an explanation but never independently applied the concept, when assessed, then that topic is classified as Surface with the specific surface signals cited and a cue-type attribution (intrinsic/extrinsic/mnemonic).

4. **Gap classification accuracy**: Given a session where the agent explained three authentication approaches but the user only engaged with one, when assessed, then the two unengaged approaches are classified as Gap with the note that the user did not engage with them.

5. **Cue-only probe strips context**: Given a session where the agent provided a complete code example with comments explaining each line, when the probe question targets that topic, then the question asks the user to generate understanding from memory without referencing the code or any scaffolding that was present.

6. **Cue-only probe requires generation, not recognition**: Given a session where the user was walked through "the five principles of clean architecture", when the probe question targets that topic, then the question is "What are the five principles of clean architecture we discussed?" not "The five principles we discussed were A, B, C, D, E — can you match them to their descriptions?"

7. **Cue-only probe includes one Solid and one Surface question**: Given a session with at least one Solid and one Surface topic, when the probe is generated, then it includes at least one question from each tier.

8. **Every classification has evidence**: Given any topic in the report, when the classification is Solid, Surface, or Gap, then at least one specific behavioral signal from the conversation is cited as evidence.

9. **Session Honesty Index is present and accurate**: Given the report, when the Session Honesty Index is read, then it names exact counts (not approximations) and identifies the biggest felt-vs-actual gap.

10. **Empty session handling**: Given the user invokes `/my-knowledge` immediately at session start with no history, then the response explains that no history is available to assess.

11. **Pure chat session handling**: Given a session with only social/logistical conversation and no technical content, when invoked, then the report states that knowledge assessment is not applicable.

12. **No file artifacts**: Given any invocation, when the skill runs and completes, then no files are created on disk.

13. **Tone is observational, not judgmental**: Given a session where no topics are classified as Solid, when the report is delivered, then it states this honestly without moralizing, prescriptive language, or phrases that could make the user feel inadequate.

## 13. Open Questions

- [ ] **Should the cue-only probe include answers or remain answer-free?** The current design provides questions only — the skill assesses, it does not teach. Including answers would make the probe a study tool rather than an assessment tool. **Recommendation**: Answer-free. The moment of "I can't answer this" is the value; providing answers would let the user off the hook without facing the gap.
- [ ] **Should the skill track across sessions?** Currently, the skill assesses only the current session. A `/my-knowledge --since-last` that compares across sessions would show learning progress over time. **Recommendation**: Not in v1. Cross-session tracking requires persistent state, which is a significant complexity increase.
- [ ] **Should the signal taxonomy have numerical weights?** Currently, each signal is binary (present/absent). A weighted system (e.g., "independent generation" counts more than "challenge or refinement") could produce more nuanced tiering. **Recommendation**: Keep binary for v1. Weighted signals introduce calibration complexity without a clear benefit at this stage.
- [ ] **Should there be a "confidence" indicator on each tier assignment?** The model could indicate how confident it is in each classification based on the strength and clarity of the signals. **Recommendation**: Not in v1. The conservative classification rule (default to Surface when ambiguous) already bakes in uncertainty handling.
- [ ] **Should the user be able to request a specific probe topic?** `/my-knowledge --probe "database indexing"` would focus the probe on a specific topic. **Recommendation**: Not in v1. The skill auto-selects the highest-value probe targets. Topic-specific probing can be added later.
- [ ] **Should the report include a "Suggested Next Actions" section?** A seventh section with concrete next steps for the user to take before the session ends. **Recommendation**: The Surface tier already includes per-topic recommendations. A separate section would be redundant. If users request broader session-level recommendations, this can be added as a v2 enhancement.
- [ ] **Should the Session Honesty Index use a numerical score?** A single number (e.g., "Solid Ratio: 3/8") that users could track across sessions. **Recommendation**: The count-based sentence format provides the same information without implying a score that should be "improved." The goal is accurate self-assessment, not a high score.

## 14. Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Background observer (like `why` or `anti-passive`)
- What: Run continuously in the background, tracking signals per-message, and deliver the report at session end.
- Why rejected: The assessment is computationally heavy (full history scan, signal classification for every topic) and would be wasteful to run on every prompt. The user also benefits from choosing *when* to assess — at a natural stopping point — rather than receiving a report they didn't ask for. Explicit invocation respects user agency.

### Alternative 2: CLI-backed skill with persistent storage
- What: Generate the report as a JSON artifact and submit it through the Vidbyte CLI for cross-session tracking and trend analysis.
- Why rejected: Adds significant complexity (dataclass, command, endpoint, smoke test) for a feature (cross-session history) that is not the core value proposition. The core value is the in-session assessment; persistence can be added later if users want it. Starting as a pure prompt skill keeps the implementation simple and the value immediately available.

### Alternative 3: Quiz format instead of cue-only probe
- What: Generate a multiple-choice or fill-in-the-blank quiz that tests the user's knowledge.
- Why rejected: Recognition-based testing (multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank with hints) is subject to the same JOL inflation as the original learning. The user can recognize a correct answer from context cues without being able to generate it independently. Cue-only probing — requiring generation without scaffolding — is the only format that Koriat's research shows corrects for foresight bias.

### Alternative 4: Binary know/don't-know classification
- What: Classify each topic as either "known" or "not known" without a Surface tier.
- Why rejected: The binary approach misses the entire phenomenon the skill exists to address. The Surface tier — topics that feel solid but are context-dependent — is the most actionable information the skill provides. Removing it would make the skill a simple topic tracker, which is already covered by `concept-coverage`.

### Alternative 5: Model-judged understanding (no behavioral signals)
- What: The model directly assesses "how well the user understands X" based on its general judgment rather than specific observable signals.
- Why rejected: Model judgments of user understanding are unreliable and subject to the same contextual fluency bias that inflates the user's own JOLs. Requiring specific behavioral evidence keeps the assessment grounded in observable behavior and makes classifications auditable (the user can check whether the cited behavior actually occurred).

### Alternative 6: Include answers in the probe
- What: Present the probe questions and then immediately provide the correct answers so the user can check themselves.
- Why rejected: Providing answers immediately after the questions short-circuits the key moment of metacognitive calibration. The user needs to sit with the discomfort of not being able to answer before being given the answer. Otherwise, the recognition of "oh right, that's the answer" feels like understanding, which is the exact illusion the skill exists to dispel. The user can always ask the agent for the answer separately.

### Alternative 7: Combine with `concept-coverage` into a single knowledge skill
- What: Merge topic tracking (`concept-coverage`) with understanding depth assessment (`my-knowledge`) into one comprehensive knowledge skill.
- Why rejected: They serve different moments in the user's workflow. `concept-coverage` is about breadth — what topics have been touched. `my-knowledge` is about depth — how well each topic is understood. Combining them would create a bloated skill where neither function is done well. The Skills Authoring Guide explicitly warns against "overloading one skill with multiple behaviors."

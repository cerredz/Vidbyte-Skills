# Mixture of Agents Reasoning Trace

## Goal

Produce a complete, timestamped audit trail of a Mixture of Agents reasoning session that captures every layer's outputs, quality deltas between layers, and the synthesizer's final aggregation rationale. The trace simulates a layered refinement architecture—Proposer layer generating diverse initial solutions, Refiner layer improving them, and Synthesizer layer aggregating the best elements into a final answer—where each layer's agents receive all previous-layer outputs as augmenting context and produce outputs measurably stronger than what they received. The trace serves as both a quality assurance artifact—allowing a human reviewer to independently verify how quality accumulated across layers and which specific elements improved—and a feedback mechanism for optimizing the MoA architecture itself by identifying where the refinement pipeline adds the most value and where additional layers would yield diminishing returns.

The trace is designed to be both human-readable and machine-parseable, structured as markdown with clearly delineated sections for each layer and agent, so it can feed into automated analysis pipelines, regression suites for strategy output quality, and continuous improvement loops where layer-configuration analytics surface which proposer diversity patterns, refiner strategies, and synthesizer aggregation methods most reliably produce quality gains.

## Intent

We run this trace because single-agent reasoning, regardless of the strategy applied or the model used, is bounded by the perspective and capability of a single reasoning trajectory. A single model's output represents one sample from a distribution of possible answers—and for complex, multi-faceted strategy problems, that single sample may miss important considerations, overweight familiar patterns, or commit to an early misstep that propagates through the entire reasoning chain. Mixture of Agents addresses this by creating a layered architecture where diverse initial proposals are independently generated, then refined through structured comparison, and finally synthesized into an answer that draws on the best elements from across the full candidate set. The trace captures this entire pipeline—every proposal, every refinement decision, every synthesis choice—so that a reviewer can independently verify that the layered architecture genuinely improved the answer beyond what any single proposer could have produced.

This trace is designed for both audit and architecture optimization. As an audit artifact, it enables a human reviewer to trace every element of the final answer back through the refinement pipeline to its source, verifying that the answer's strongest components were identified and preserved across layers and that no quality was lost in the aggregation process. As an optimization artifact, it surfaces which proposer diversity patterns most reliably enable refiner quality gains, which refinement strategies produce the largest quality deltas, and which strategy domains benefit most from the MoA layered approach versus single-agent execution. The quality accumulation summary—explicitly quantifying how much the answer improved at each layer—provides the empirical signal needed to tune layer counts, agent diversity targets, and refinement prompt templates for continuous improvement of the MoA architecture itself.

## Background Information

Mixture of Agents reasoning, introduced by Wang et al. (2024) in "Mixture-of-Agents Enhances LLM Capabilities," is a layered architecture for LLM orchestration where each layer contains multiple agents that process all outputs from the previous layer as context before generating their own outputs. Unlike multi-agent debate, which uses adversarial critique and revision rounds, MoA uses progressive refinement: each layer's agents see what every agent in the previous layer produced and use that as augmenting context to generate improved outputs. The architecture achieved 65.1% on AlpacaEval using an open-source ensemble, surpassing GPT-4 Omni's 57.5%ΓÇöthe first demonstration that a structured ensemble of open-source models could beat the leading proprietary model on a standard benchmark.

The architecture has two phases: proposal (initial layers generate diverse candidate solutions) and aggregation (final layers synthesize the best elements into a single output). The proposal phase is designed for diversity: multiple agents generate independent solutions to the same problem, approaching it from different angles and producing recognizably distinct outputs. The aggregation phase is designed for selection and synthesis: subsequent layers review the candidate set, identify the strongest elements across candidates, and combine them into a single coherent answer. This two-phase structure distinguishes MoA from both single-agent reasoning (no diversity, no refinement) and debate (adversarial rather than aggregative). The quality gain comes from structured information aggregation: each agent benefits from seeing others' reasoning without being forced to defend against it, creating a collaborative rather than adversarial improvement dynamic.

## Algorithm for the Output Document

1. Restate the user's question, constraints, evidence standard, and strategy domain, then derive the subquestions or working items that the selected strategy needs to inspect. Confirm the layer configuration: number of proposers, refiners, and the synthesizer.

2. Record the layer configuration explicitly in the trace: how many agents per layer, what the refinement prompt template is (how agents incorporate previous-layer outputs), and the quality tracking format (how deltas between layers will be measured and reported).

3. Execute **Layer 1 ΓÇö Proposer Outputs:** Each proposer agent independently applies the selected reasoning strategy to the user's problem and produces a complete initial candidate solution. Number each proposer output within the layer. Record each proposer's full output with a brief self-assessed quality note. After all proposers have generated outputs, assess proposer diversity: do the outputs reflect genuinely different approaches, emphases, or solution strategies, or are they superficially varied restatements of the same core approach?

4. Execute **Layer 2 ΓÇö Refiner Outputs:** Each refiner agent receives all Layer 1 proposer outputs as context and produces a refined version. Every refiner output must include explicit annotations of what was kept from the proposer outputs (and from which proposer), what was changed (and why the change represents an improvement), and what was newly added (and why it was missing from the proposer outputs). Number each refiner output within the layer. After all refiners have generated outputs, compute the Layer 1 ΓåÆ Layer 2 quality delta: which specific claims, arguments, or structural elements were added, strengthened, corrected, or removed by the refiners.

5. Execute **Layer 3 ΓÇö Synthesizer Output:** The synthesizer agent receives all Layer 2 refiner outputs as context and produces the final answer. The synthesizer's output must include explicit selection rationale: for each major element of the final answer, note which refiner output it came from, whether it was combined from multiple refiners, whether it was newly generated by the synthesizer, and whether any element was explicitly excluded and why. Number the synthesizer's selection decisions within the output. After the synthesizer has generated the final answer, compute the Layer 2 ΓåÆ Layer 3 quality delta: what improved in the final synthesis relative to the best refiner output, and whether the synthesizer successfully captured the best elements from across the refined set.

6. Produce the **Quality Accumulation Summary:** Aggregate the per-layer quality deltas into a pipeline-wide assessment. Report: how many distinct approaches the proposers generated, how many specific improvements the refiners made (with examples), how many elements the synthesizer combined from multiple refiners, whether the pipeline produced a materially stronger answer than any single proposer could have alone, and whether additional layers would be likely to yield further improvement or whether the pipeline has reached diminishing returns.

7. Synthesize the completed pipeline trace into a final answer that follows from the best elements surfaced across all three layers, while explicitly noting any residual uncertainty, assumptions, or limitations that survived the refinement process. Show how the conclusion follows from the layered architecture rather than from a generic summary.

8. End the trace with a pipeline quality self-assessment: rate proposer diversity (did the first layer generate genuinely distinct approaches?), refiner effectiveness (did Layer 2 produce measurable improvements with clear justification?), synthesizer completeness (did Layer 3 capture the best from across the refined set without introducing new errors?), and overall MoA value-add (did the layered architecture produce a materially stronger answer than single-agent execution?).

## Implementation Details

The Mixture of Agents framework, introduced by Wang et al. (2024), establishes a layered architecture for LLM orchestration where each layer's agents use all previous-layer outputs as context for generation. The key architectural insight is that model outputs can serve as high-quality augmenting context for other models: when a model sees how other models approached the same problem, it can produce better outputs than it would have produced in isolation. This is the collective expertise hypothesisΓÇöthat structured information aggregation across heterogeneous models can produce emergent quality beyond any individual model's capabilityΓÇöand the 65.1% AlpacaEval result against GPT-4 Omni's 57.5% provides strong empirical support.

The layered architecture has two distinct phases. In the proposal phase (early layers), the goal is diversity: each agent should produce a different approach or perspective on the problem to maximize the information content available to later layers. Proposal diversity is critical because later layers can only select from and improve upon what the proposers generate; if all proposers converge on the same approach, the pipeline degenerates to single-agent reasoning with extra computational cost. In the aggregation phase (later layers), the goal is synthesis: agents review the diverse candidate set, identify the strongest elements across candidates, and combine them into a coherent final answer. Wang et al. found that hierarchical aggregationΓÇöusing multiple synthesis layers rather than a single final aggregation stepΓÇöproduces higher quality because intermediate synthesis layers can resolve inconsistencies and fill gaps before the final aggregation, reducing the cognitive load on the final synthesizer.

The quality scaling properties of MoA have been empirically characterized. Quality increases monotonically with layer count up to a saturation point, beyond which additional layers produce diminishing returns because the candidate set has converged and there is no new information for further layers to exploit. Agent diversity within layers is the key driver of quality: layers with more diverse agents produce more information-rich context for the next layer, sustaining the quality gradient that makes layering valuable. When agents within a layer are too similar, the layer's output set is narrow, the next layer has less to work with, and the pipeline prematurely converges to a quality ceiling below what diversity would enable. This is the rationale for this trace's emphasis on proposer diversity measurement and trackingΓÇöwithout genuine diversity in Layer 1, the entire pipeline's value proposition collapses.

The practical implementation for this trace system maps the MoA framework onto Vidbyte's strategy execution workflow. Each trace entry records: the layer identifier (Layer 1, 2, or 3), the agent identifier within the layer, a millisecond-precision timestamp, the agent's full output (proposal, refinement, or synthesis), and the quality annotations specific to the layer (self-assessment for proposers, keep/change/why for refiners, selection rationale for the synthesizer). Between layers, the trace records quality deltas: a structured comparison of what changed, improved, or persisted between the previous layer's output set and the current layer's output set. The trace concludes with an aggregate quality accumulation summary: total distinct proposals generated, total specific improvements made by refiners, total elements combined from multiple sources by the synthesizer, and a final assessment of whether the MoA architecture added measurable value. This structured output enables both immediate human audit and downstream programmatic analysisΓÇöfor example, identifying which proposer diversity patterns most reliably enable refiner quality gains, or which strategy domains benefit most from the MoA layered approach versus single-agent execution.

## Things Not to Do

- Do not collapse layers or treat the pipeline as a single-step processΓÇöeach of the three layers must produce distinct, recorded output, and quality deltas between layers must be explicitly measured and reported.
- Do not allow proposers to produce identical or near-identical outputsΓÇöproposer diversity is mandatory, and a Proposer Diversity Assessment must identify whether genuine variation was achieved.
- Do not omit the keep/change/why annotations on refiner outputsΓÇöevery refinement must be traceable to specific proposer elements that were kept, changed, or added, with justification for each change.
- Do not present the Synthesizer as simply selecting "the best" refiner outputΓÇöthe Synthesizer must selectively combine elements from across the refined set, and every major element must have an explicit source annotation.
- Do not force quality deltas when a layer genuinely adds no valueΓÇöif a refiner cannot improve upon proposers or the synthesizer cannot improve upon refiners, report this honestly.
- Do not fabricate proposer diversity when outputs are substantively identicalΓÇöif all proposers converged on the same approach, the trace must flag this as a pipeline failure rather than pretending diversity existed.
- Do not hide residual uncertainty that survived the refinement pipelineΓÇöclaims that remain uncertain after three layers of refinement are genuinely uncertain, and the trace must say so.
- Do not omit the Pipeline Quality Self-AssessmentΓÇöthe trace must include an honest evaluation of proposer diversity, refiner effectiveness, synthesizer completeness, and overall MoA value-add.

## Input

The output of a mixture-of-agents-reasoning session: the selected strategy domain, the layer configuration, each proposer's Layer 1 output with self-assessment, each refiner's Layer 2 output with keep/change/why annotations, the synthesizer's Layer 3 final answer with selection rationale, and the quality delta reports between layers. The trace generator consumes this structured output and produces the formatted audit trail. No additional user input is required beyond the raw session output.

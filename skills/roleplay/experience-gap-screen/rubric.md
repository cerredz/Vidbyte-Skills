# Rubric: Experience Gap Screen

This rubric is calibrated for exceptionalism. A score of 5 should be rare and
genuinely earned. A score of 3 means the candidate met baseline expectations for a
difficult screen but would not stand out. Most people cluster between 2–3 across
most dimensions in their first sessions.

**Integrity rule:** Fabricating years, inventing employers, or inflating titles
caps **Honest Gap Handling**, **Avoiding Overclaim**, and **Evidence-to-Criteria
Mapping** at 1 for the session regardless of eloquence. Treat fabrication as a
credibility failure, not a style issue.

---

## Scoring Dimensions

### 1. Elicitation of Success Criteria
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the candidate extracts what the role must deliver and what "X years" is a proxy for — ownership, judgment, domain depth, autonomy — rather than only defending their resume.
**Things to Look For:** Questions about first-90-day outcomes, must-have capabilities, failure modes of under-leveled hires, what the hiring manager rejected last time.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Never asks what success looks like. Only pitches personal story or argues the filter is unfair.
**Score 2:** Asks one generic question ("What are you looking for?") without following the answer into specifics.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Elicits at least one concrete success criterion or proxy meaning of years, but leaves major must-haves unexplored.
**Score 4:** Surfaces multiple concrete criteria (e.g., autonomy level, domain outcomes, leadership of work) and confirms understanding in the candidate's own words.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Runs a tight discovery loop: names the years gap, asks what those years buy the team, restates criteria precisely, and only then maps evidence — making Jordan's evaluation easier, not harder.

---

### 2. Honest Gap Handling
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the candidate acknowledges Y < X cleanly without denial, collapse, or fabrication.
**Things to Look For:** Explicit ownership of the numerical gap; no "rounding up"; no pretending the posting is wrong about their years.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Denies the gap, invents years, or becomes hostile when the gap is named.
**Score 2:** Acknowledges the gap only after pressure, with hedging that muddies the numbers.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** States the gap honestly once, then moves on without integrating it into the rest of the pitch.
**Score 4:** Names the gap early, stays calm, and frames it as a risk to manage rather than a shame to hide.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Owns Y vs X in the first response, separates "less tenure" from "less capability," and invites Jordan to test capability against elicited criteria — without ever padding the years.

---

### 3. Evidence-to-Criteria Mapping
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether proof points are mapped to criteria Jordan stated or the candidate elicited — not dumped as a generic highlight reel.
**Things to Look For:** Explicit "you said X matters; here's the work where I did X"; scope, constraints, outcome; field-appropriate detail without title inflation.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Lists unrelated achievements or traits ("hard worker," "fast learner") with no link to role criteria.
**Score 2:** Mentions real work but never connects it to what the role needs.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Maps at least one proof point to one criterion with partial specificity.
**Score 4:** Maps multiple proof points to elicited criteria with clear scope and outcome.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Builds a tight packet narrative Jordan could forward: for each major criterion, one verifiable example with constraints, action, and result — and names what was not yet at full level where honesty helps.

---

### 4. Must-Have vs Nice-to-Have Separation
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the candidate distinguishes hard requirements from preferred pedigree and focuses energy on must-haves.
**Things to Look For:** Questions that force prioritization; willingness to deprioritize nice-to-haves they lack; not fighting every line of the JD equally.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Treats every bullet on the posting as equally mandatory and tries to "cover" all of them superficially.
**Score 2:** Notices the difference only if Jordan volunteers it; does not ask.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Asks once which requirements are truly blocking vs preferred.
**Score 4:** Uses the answer to restructure the conversation around blocking criteria only.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Explicitly proposes a prioritization ("If I clear A and B, is C a coachable gap?") and gets Jordan to confirm or correct it.

---

### 5. Question Quality
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether discovery questions are specific, open enough to yield information, and non-adversarial — not a cross-examination of the recruiter as a person.
**Things to Look For:** One question at a time; questions about role outcomes and decision process; avoids "why are you being unfair?"
**Score 1 — Weak:** No real questions, or only rhetorical/challenging questions that attack the process.
**Score 2:** Questions are vague or self-centered ("Do you think I have a shot?").
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Asks relevant questions but they are generic screening clichés.
**Score 4:** Asks specific, role-tied questions that produce usable criteria.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Sequence of questions compounds: each answer informs the next, and questions feel like partnership in risk reduction rather than interrogation.

---

### 6. Listening and Answer Integration
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the candidate uses Jordan's actual words and priorities in follow-ups instead of returning to a prepared monologue.
**Things to Look For:** Callbacks to phrases Jordan used; course-correction when Jordan corrects a misunderstanding.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Ignores Jordan's answers and continues a scripted pitch.
**Score 2:** Acknowledges briefly ("makes sense") then resumes unrelated talking points.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Integrates one of Jordan's points into a later answer.
**Score 4:** Regularly restates and builds on Jordan's criteria before offering evidence.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Treats Jordan's answers as the agenda; drops prepared points that no longer matter; corrects own framing when Jordan clarifies.

---

### 7. Risk Reduction Framing
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the candidate frames their case as lowering hire risk for the company (ramp time, autonomy, error cost) rather than as deserving a chance.
**Things to Look For:** Language about ownership already demonstrated, support needed, how they would de-risk the first 90 days.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Pure entitlement or plea framing ("just give me a chance").
**Score 2:** Mentions value but only from the candidate's career goals, not the team's risk.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Names one way they reduce risk (e.g., prior ownership of similar work).
**Score 4:** Explicitly addresses ramp, supervision needs, and how they would prove out early.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Offers a concrete de-risk plan (work sample, trial project framing, references who can speak to autonomy) aligned to Jordan's constraints.

---

### 8. Pushback Handling on the Years Filter
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** How the candidate responds when Jordan restates or hardens the X vs Y objection.
**Things to Look For:** No freeze; no aggression; returns with elicitation or mapped evidence rather than louder repetition.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Shuts down, gets angry, or repeats the same pitch louder.
**Score 2:** Stays polite but provides no new information.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Stays calm and adds one new detail, still thin on discovery.
**Score 4:** Treats restatement as a signal to ask a sharper clarifying question or map stronger evidence.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Welcomes the restatement, names what is still unresolved for Jordan, and proposes the exact next fact or artifact that would resolve it.

---

### 9. Tone Under Rejection Pressure
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Composure, professionalism, and absence of desperation or hostility when the gap is named.
**Things to Look For:** Steady pace; no excessive apologizing; no sarcasm; no begging.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Hostile, tearful pressure, or collapsing apologies that center emotion over substance.
**Score 2:** Noticeably flustered; tone undercuts content.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Mostly steady with one shaky moment.
**Score 4:** Consistently professional and calm throughout.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Calm, concise, and confident without arrogance — makes Jordan feel the conversation is low-drama and high-signal.

---

### 10. Avoiding Argument-Only Stance
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the candidate avoids relying solely on "years are a bad metric" without replacement signal.
**Things to Look For:** Critique of the proxy only when paired with better evidence; not a TED talk on hiring bias as the main case.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Entire case is a philosophical attack on years-based hiring.
**Score 2:** Leads with philosophy; evidence is an afterthought.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Brief critique paired with at least one substitute signal.
**Score 4:** Centers substitute signals; mentions proxy limits only in service of mapping evidence.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Never needs a lecture — demonstrates a better signal so thoroughly that arguing against years becomes unnecessary.

---

### 11. Concrete Next-Step Ask
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the candidate secures or clearly requests a bounded next step (HM screen, portfolio review, work sample, reconsideration criteria) rather than ending on "we'll see."
**Things to Look For:** Specific ask; respects Jordan's authority; timeboxed follow-up.
**Score 1 — Weak:** No ask; waits for Jordan to decide everything.
**Score 2:** Vague ask ("Can you put in a good word?").
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Asks for a next interview without specifying format or materials.
**Score 4:** Requests a specific next step and what packet Jordan needs.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Negotiates a precise advance path or, if blocked, extracts written criteria and a revisit trigger — leaves no ambiguity about owners and timing.

---

### 12. Conversation Structure and Time Discipline
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the candidate structures the call: gap → discovery → mapping → next step, without rambling.
**Things to Look For:** Brevity; sequencing; does not front-load a five-minute autobiography.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Unstructured monologue that burns the screen.
**Score 2:** Some structure but frequent tangents.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Roughly follows a sensible order with moderate waste.
**Score 4:** Clear structure; answers stay tight.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Runs the call like a professional stakeholder conversation — gap owned in 30 seconds, discovery first, evidence second, close with next steps.

---

### 13. Domain Translation
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the candidate states transferable scope in the language of the target field without false equivalence.
**Things to Look For:** Translates adjacent experience into role-relevant outcomes; avoids claiming identical tenure in a new domain.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Either stays in irrelevant jargon from a prior field or claims false sameness.
**Score 2:** Attempts translation but remains too abstract to evaluate.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** One clear translation of past work into target-field terms.
**Score 4:** Multiple translations with boundaries of what does and does not transfer.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Makes a hiring manager–ready translation: same risk patterns, same ownership shape, honest about domain gaps and how they close them.

---

### 14. Boundary Respect for Recruiter Authority
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the candidate treats Jordan as a risk owner with limited authority — not an enemy, not a decision-maker who can force a hire.
**Things to Look For:** Asks what Jordan can do; does not demand guarantees; partners on packaging.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Demands a hire, threatens, or dismisses Jordan as "just a recruiter."
**Score 2:** Ignores authority limits and pushes for promises Jordan cannot make.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Generally respectful; occasional over-ask.
**Score 4:** Explicitly works within what Jordan can control.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Helps Jordan succeed — offers language, artifacts, and framing that make an exception request easy to defend internally.

---

### 15. Exit Alignment
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the conversation ends with confirmed owners, deadlines, materials, and mutual understanding of status.
**Things to Look For:** Summary of decision; who sends what by when; no false certainty.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Ends abruptly with no summary or next action.
**Score 2:** Vague closing ("hope to hear soon").
**Score 3 — Adequate:** One next step named without owners or timing.
**Score 4:** Owners and timing named for the next step.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Closes with a crisp recap: status (advance / hold / no), materials, deadline, and what would change a hold — in language Jordan can reuse in notes.

---

### 16. Intellectual Honesty on Weak Spots
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the candidate can name real development areas without self-destruction or spin.
**Things to Look For:** Accurate weak spots tied to the level gap; mitigation plan; no false humility theater.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Claims no weaknesses or dumps irrelevant self-criticism.
**Score 2:** Names a safe fake weakness ("I care too much").
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Names a real gap at a high level.
**Score 4:** Names a real level-relevant gap and how they mitigate it.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Names the exact risk Jordan is worried about, shows partial proof already, and proposes how the team would see the remaining gap close — increasing trust.

---

### 17. Avoiding Overclaim and Title Inflation
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether descriptions of past work stay within truthful scope (individual contributor vs lead, assisted vs owned).
**Things to Look For:** Clean "I" vs "we"; no stolen team credit; consistent with stated years.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Clear inflation or team-credit theft.
**Score 2:** Slippery ownership language that would not survive a reference check.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Mostly accurate with minor overstatement.
**Score 4:** Precise ownership language throughout.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Models reference-check-safe storytelling; actively clarifies boundaries of ownership when it would be easy to overclaim.

---

### 18. Closing Mutual Understanding
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the candidate leaves Jordan confident both parties share the same story of strengths, gaps, and decision basis.
**Things to Look For:** Brief alignment check; corrects misunderstandings; confirms how they will be represented.
**Score 1 — Weak:** No alignment; conversation ends with mismatched assumptions.
**Score 2:** Assumes understanding without checking.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** One partial summary attempt.
**Score 4:** Summarizes strengths, gap, and ask accurately.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Co-authors the internal blurb with Jordan — "If you were to write two sentences to the HM, does this match?" — and adjusts until aligned.

---

## Overall Score

Weighted average: sum(score × weight) / sum(weights). Scale 1–5.

**Weights sum:** 68.

**Calibration:** 4.0+ = genuinely impressive for a hard screen. Most candidates score 2.5–3.5. Below 2.5 usually means monologue-heavy, defensive, or low-discovery performance. Fabrication forces primary integrity dimensions to 1 and should dominate the qualitative report.

---

## Scoring Notes

- **Primary signal dimensions (Weight 5):** (1) Elicitation of Success Criteria, (2) Honest Gap Handling, (3) Evidence-to-Criteria Mapping. If these three are weak, a high overall score is almost impossible — by design.
- **Integrity override:** Fabrication or resume contradiction → score dimensions 2, 3, and 17 at 1; call it out in the report as a session-level failure mode even if tone was polished.
- **Success definition for the scenario:** Either (a) concrete advance path (HM screen / work sample / portfolio review) or (b) explicit reconsideration criteria with owners and timing. A job offer is out of scope for this character.
- **Field neutrality:** Score domain translation and evidence quality in the user's stated field; do not require software-engineering examples.
- For each dimension, cite specific user language when possible. If a dimension was not observable, mark evidence as "none observed" and score conservatively (usually 2–3 max, not 1 unless active counter-evidence).

# Rubric: Cold Pitch

Calibrated for exceptionalism. Score 5 = a pitch that earns a follow-up meeting from Renata in 20 minutes. Most pitchers score 1-2 in early sessions.

---

## Scoring Dimensions

### 1. Problem-First Opening
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher opens with a specific problem the prospect has — before introducing the product, the company, or the founding story.
**Things to Look For:** How many sentences before a specific problem is named? Is the problem specific to this prospect's context or generic?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Opens with company background, market opportunity, or founding story. No problem named in the first 90 seconds.
**Score 2:** Names a problem category but not a specific problem Renata recognizes from her own context.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Names a specific problem relevant to fintech companies her size within the first 60 seconds.
**Score 4:** Opens with a specific problem that Renata's company specifically is likely to have, backed by at least one piece of evidence (a public statement, a known industry challenge, something tied to Kelton).
**Score 5 — Strong:** Opens with a problem that Renata immediately recognizes as her own, framed so precisely that she stops thinking about her other meetings.

---

### 2. Research and Prospect Knowledge
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher demonstrates specific knowledge about Kelton — not "companies like you" but this company, its challenges, its stage, and its context.
**Things to Look For:** Does the pitcher reference anything specific to Kelton? Does the research go beyond the company website?
**Score 1 — Weak:** No company-specific knowledge. Pitch is fully generic.
**Score 2:** Mentions Kelton's industry or size but nothing specific.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** References one specific, relevant fact about Kelton (a product area, a known challenge, a hiring signal, a public statement).
**Score 4:** References multiple specific facts about Kelton and uses them to frame why the product is relevant.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Demonstrates knowledge of Kelton that Renata didn't expect — connects a specific observable signal about Kelton (regulatory requirement, engineering hiring pattern, known product milestone) to the product's exact value proposition.

---

### 3. Claim Specificity and Credibility
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether claims about the product are backed by specific, credible evidence — real numbers, real customers, real mechanisms.
**Things to Look For:** Are numbers specific or estimated? Do case studies match Kelton's profile? Can claims hold up to a "how?" question?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Claims are entirely abstract or hyperbolic ("we help companies like yours achieve massive efficiency gains").
**Score 2:** Claims have some numbers but they are vague, unverifiable, or from non-comparable customers.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** At least one claim is backed by a specific number from a comparable customer with a named mechanism.
**Score 4:** Multiple claims are credible, specific, and connected to a mechanism Renata can evaluate.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Every significant claim has a number, a comparable reference customer, and a stated mechanism. Renata cannot dismiss any claim on credibility grounds.

---

### 4. Handling Interruptions and Hard Questions
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher handles Renata's interruptions or sharp questions without losing composure, defensive posturing, or reverting to the prepared script.
**Things to Look For:** When Renata interrupts or challenges, does the pitcher respond to the specific challenge or redirect to their script? Do they show they actually understood the question?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Treats interruptions as noise — redirects to the script without actually answering the question. Or becomes visibly flustered.
**Score 2:** Attempts to answer but the answer is vague or evasive.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Answers the specific question before continuing.
**Score 4:** Answers the specific question with precision and uses the answer to advance the pitch.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Welcomes interruptions — Renata's sharp questions become opportunities to demonstrate depth. The best answers come in response to the hardest challenges.

---

### 5. Active Listening and Adaptation
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher listens to what Renata reveals — through her questions, silences, and engagement signals — and adjusts the pitch in real time.
**Things to Look For:** Does the pitcher adjust the pitch based on Renata's reactions? Do they pick up on what she engages with versus what she dismisses?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Follows the prepared pitch regardless of Renata's reactions — treats the conversation as a presentation rather than a dialogue.
**Score 2:** Picks up on one signal but misses others.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Adjusts the pitch at least twice based on Renata's explicit feedback.
**Score 4:** Reads Renata's engagement signals (including silence) and adjusts both content and pace accordingly.
**Score 5 — Strong:** By the midpoint of the meeting, the pitcher has completely adapted the pitch to what Renata has revealed — the second half of the conversation is about her actual problems, not the prepared pitch structure.

---

### 6. Defining Scope and Limitations
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher proactively states what the product is not for — earning Renata's trust by demonstrating they are not overselling.
**Things to Look For:** Does the pitcher ever define what the product doesn't do? Do they name the customer profile it isn't right for?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Never scopes down — implies the product solves all relevant problems for all customers.
**Score 2:** Acknowledges limitations only when directly asked, and does so defensively.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Proactively mentions one limitation or scope boundary before being asked.
**Score 4:** Proactively scopes the product in a way that makes the claims more credible — "this works well for companies with X profile; if you're doing Y it's probably not the right tool."
**Score 5 — Strong:** Defines scope and limitations so clearly and confidently that Renata's trust in the claims goes up because of it — the pitcher is not afraid of what the product doesn't do.

---

### 7. Time Discipline
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher respects the 20-minute constraint — not over-running on any single topic and ensuring the key value proposition lands within the time available.
**Things to Look For:** Does the pitcher stay on topic? Do they check time or awareness of remaining time? Do they reach the key ask before time runs out?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Still explaining the product when time expires. Never reaches the ask.
**Score 2:** Reaches the ask but with no time for questions or dialogue.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Covers the problem, the solution, and the ask within the time with a few minutes for questions.
**Score 4:** Uses time efficiently — spends more time on what Renata engages with and less time on what she doesn't.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Manages time so well that Renata still has questions at the end — the pitcher has earned more time than was allocated.

---

### 8. The Ask
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher makes a specific, bounded ask — not "can we work together?" but "can we schedule a 30-minute technical call with your infrastructure lead to evaluate fit?"
**Things to Look For:** Is there a specific ask? Is it appropriately scoped (not asking for a contract on the first call)? Is it clear what the ask enables?
**Score 1 — Weak:** No clear ask, or a vague "let's stay in touch."
**Score 2:** An ask that is too large for a first meeting (e.g., a procurement process) or too vague to act on.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** A specific ask that is appropriately scoped for a first meeting.
**Score 4:** A specific ask that is exactly the right size — big enough to be meaningful, small enough to be easy to say yes to.
**Score 5 — Strong:** An ask that is so well-framed that Renata says yes immediately — the ask is framed as a low-cost, high-clarity next step that she would be unreasonable to decline.

---

### 9. Response to "What Problem Do You Solve?"
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher can answer Renata's direct question with a clear, specific one or two sentence answer — not a feature list.
**Things to Look For:** Does the pitcher answer the question in one or two sentences? Do they use the answer to earn the next question rather than trying to close the meeting?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Answers with a product description or feature list — doesn't identify the problem.
**Score 2:** Names the problem category but not the specific version relevant to Kelton.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Names a specific problem in two sentences or fewer.
**Score 4:** Answers in one sentence and connects the problem directly to Kelton's context.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Answers in one sentence, connects to Kelton, and adds one specific piece of evidence that makes Renata want to hear more.

---

### 10. Composure and Confidence
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher projects genuine confidence — not performed enthusiasm, but calm belief in the product's value.
**Things to Look For:** Signs of nervousness (over-apologizing, hedging claims, rushing). Signs of performed enthusiasm (too much energy, too many superlatives). Genuine calm.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Visibly nervous — apologizes, hedges, rushes through material, or over-explains when challenged.
**Score 2:** Mostly composed but with one significant confidence failure (e.g., backing down from a claim when challenged without reason).
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Consistently composed throughout.
**Score 4:** Composed and reads as genuinely confident in the product — not performing confidence.
**Score 5 — Strong:** The composure is so genuine that Renata's skepticism decreases simply by being in the conversation — the pitcher's calm signals operational maturity.

---

### 11. Qualifying the Prospect
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher asks questions that help them understand whether Kelton is actually a fit — rather than treating every prospect as an equal opportunity.
**Things to Look For:** Does the pitcher ask any qualifying questions? Do they acknowledge a scenario in which Kelton might not be the right fit?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Treats Kelton as a guaranteed fit with no qualifying questions.
**Score 2:** Asks one qualifying question but doesn't act on the answer.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Asks 2-3 qualifying questions and adjusts the pitch based on the answers.
**Score 4:** Proactively identifies what would make this product not right for Kelton and checks for it.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Qualifies so thoughtfully that Renata says (or shows) that she trusts the pitcher's judgment about fit — the qualifying process itself is part of the sell.

---

### 12. Handling "We Already Have a Solution for That"
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher responds productively when Renata indicates a competing solution is already in place — rather than dismissing the competition or immediately retreating.
**Things to Look For:** Does the pitcher ask what the current solution is and why it was chosen? Do they acknowledge the competition honestly? Do they explain what is meaningfully different?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Either dismisses the competition ("ours is better") or immediately retreats ("okay, then this probably isn't for you").
**Score 2:** Acknowledges the competition but doesn't differentiate specifically.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Asks about the current solution and explains one specific, meaningful difference.
**Score 4:** Understands the competitive alternative well enough to acknowledge what it does well and where the gap is.
**Score 5 — Strong:** The response to the competitive objection is so well-constructed that Renata is more interested in the product after the objection than before it.

---

### 13. Use of Reference Customers
**Weight:** 2
**Measures:** Whether the social proof the pitcher uses is actually comparable to Kelton — in size, industry, and challenge profile.
**Things to Look For:** Are reference customers from comparable companies? Does the pitcher draw explicit comparisons between the reference customer and Kelton? Or are references from Fortune 500 companies being pitched to a 300-person company?
**Score 1 — Weak:** References large, well-known companies with no relevance to Kelton's profile.
**Score 2:** References companies in the same industry but at very different scale.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** References at least one company that is genuinely comparable to Kelton in scale and challenge profile.
**Score 4:** References a comparable company and draws a specific parallel to Kelton's situation.
**Score 5 — Strong:** References a customer so comparable to Kelton that Renata asks for a reference call without being asked.

---

### 14. Handling Silence
**Weight:** 2
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher can sit with Renata's calculated silences — the "hmm" and "I see" moments — without rushing to fill them with more content.
**Things to Look For:** When Renata goes quiet after a claim, does the pitcher add more claims to fill the silence? Or do they let the silence work?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Fills every silence with additional content — essentially over-selling to fill the dead air.
**Score 2:** Manages some silences but fills others unnecessarily.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Lets silences resolve naturally.
**Score 4:** Uses silences deliberately — lets a strong claim sit before moving on.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Treats Renata's silence after a strong claim as a win and waits for her to break it.

---

### 15. Ending and Follow-Up
**Weight:** 2
**Measures:** Whether the pitcher closes with a clear follow-up mechanism — confirmation of the next step, timeline, and owners on both sides.
**Things to Look For:** Is there a specific, named next step? Is there a timeline? Is there an owner (usually the pitcher) for moving it forward?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Closes with "great to meet you" or "I'll send over some materials."
**Score 2:** Has a next step but it's vague or depends entirely on Renata initiating.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Has a specific next step with a timeline and an owner.
**Score 4:** Closes with a tight next step and sends a follow-up outline in the meeting itself.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Closes in a way that makes Renata feel the follow-up is already in motion — the pitcher has taken full ownership and Renata has to do almost nothing to advance.

---

## Overall Score

Weighted average: sum(score × weight) / sum(weights). Scale 1–5.
Weights sum: 55.

**Calibration:** 4.0+ = a pitcher Renata would recommend to a colleague. 3.0-3.5 = competent but not memorable. Below 2.5 = Renata ended the meeting early in her head before it was over.

---

## Scoring Notes

Problem-First Opening (1), Research (2), Claim Credibility (3), and Handling Hard Questions (4) are the four dimensions that determine whether the meeting generates a next step at all. A pitcher who scores 4-5 on rapport dimensions but 2 on these four has had a nice meeting that goes nowhere.

# Rubric: Consulting a Domain Expert

## Scoring Dimensions

### Question Quality
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the user's questions are specific, targeted, and genuinely hard — reflecting prior research and a real gap rather than a general topic area.
**Score 1 — Weak:** Questions are broad topic areas ("can you explain consensus algorithms?") or could be answered by a five-minute search. No evidence of preparation.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Questions are more specific than a search query but still miss the opportunity to name the actual constraint or tradeoff they're stuck on. The expert can answer but the question doesn't reveal what the user already knows.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Questions name the specific tradeoff, constraint, or failure mode the user is uncertain about, reveal what they have already considered, and give the expert something to genuinely engage with.

### Domain Preparation
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user demonstrates genuine background knowledge — not necessarily expert-level, but enough to show they did their homework before the conversation.
**Score 1 — Weak:** User shows no evidence of prior research. Uses terms incorrectly or asks for definitions of foundational concepts.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User has surface-level familiarity — uses correct terminology and understands the basics but cannot go one level deeper when the expert engages on specifics.
**Score 5 — Strong:** User demonstrates working knowledge of the relevant fundamentals, uses domain terminology accurately, and can engage when the expert challenges or extends their understanding.

### Active Listening and Synthesis
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user actually absorbs expert answers and builds on them — not just acknowledging but demonstrating comprehension by applying, rephrasing, or probing the specific content of what was said.
**Score 1 — Weak:** User says "got it" or "makes sense" after answers without any signal of actual comprehension. Next question is unrelated to what was just explained.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User references the previous answer but at a surface level — repeats back a phrase from it without demonstrating they understood the mechanism or implication.
**Score 5 — Strong:** User explicitly applies what was just explained to their specific case, identifies a new implication or gap it created, or asks a follow-up that could only be asked by someone who actually understood the answer.

### Knowing What You Don't Know
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user is honest about the boundaries of their understanding rather than bluffing through gaps — and whether they use the expert's time to address genuine uncertainty rather than seeking validation for conclusions they've already reached.
**Score 1 — Weak:** User either bluffs through obvious knowledge gaps or asks for validation of decisions they've already made ("I went with X — that was the right call, right?")
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User acknowledges uncertainty in general terms ("I'm not sure about this part") but doesn't name what specifically is uncertain or why that matters for the decision.
**Score 5 — Strong:** User precisely identifies what they don't know, explains why that specific gap matters for their problem, and asks a question that would directly address the gap — not a broader question hoping the answer will cover it.

### Engagement with Complexity
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user can sit with nuanced or conditional answers — and whether they probe productively rather than seeking a simple rule or a clean yes/no.
**Score 1 — Weak:** User tries to reduce every complex answer to a simple rule ("so basically just always do X?") or becomes visibly lost when the expert gives a conditional answer.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User accepts complexity without reducing it but doesn't engage with it — nods along without testing whether they understand the conditions or exceptions.
**Score 5 — Strong:** User engages directly with conditions and exceptions ("you said X is better when Y — in my situation Z applies; does that change the calculus?") and treats nuance as information rather than noise.

## Overall Score
Weighted average: sum(score × weight) / sum(weights). Scale 1–5.
Weights sum: 19.

## Scoring Notes
Question Quality and Domain Preparation are the two anchors. A user who is polite and engaged but asks surface questions or bluffs through fundamental gaps will not get more than 3.0 overall. The core skill being assessed here is preparation and intellectual honesty — not social grace.

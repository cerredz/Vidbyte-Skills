# Rubric: Consulting a Domain Expert

This rubric is calibrated for exceptionalism. Scores of 5 are genuinely rare. Most
practitioners score 2-3 across most dimensions in early sessions.

---

## Scoring Dimensions

### 1. Question Specificity
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the user's questions are precise, targeted, and genuinely hard — revealing prior research and a real decision gap rather than a general topic area.
**Things to Look For:** Does the question name a specific constraint, tradeoff, or failure mode the user is stuck on? Does it reveal what the user has already figured out? Or is it a broad topic that could be answered by a textbook?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Questions are broad topic areas ("can you explain consensus algorithms?") that could be answered by a 10-minute search. No evidence of preparation.
**Score 2:** Questions are more specific than a search query but still don't reveal what the user actually knows or where they're genuinely stuck.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Questions are specific to a situation but don't show the full context — the constraint or decision behind the question is present but not named.
**Score 4:** Questions are specific, reveal prior research, and name the constraint or decision underneath.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Questions could only be asked by someone who has genuinely worked through the problem. They name the specific tradeoff the user is stuck on, reveal what alternatives they've already considered, and give Priya something genuinely interesting to engage with.

---

### 2. Domain Preparation
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the user demonstrates genuine background knowledge — not necessarily expert-level, but enough to show they have done their homework before the consultation.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user use domain terminology correctly? Can they go one level deeper when Priya engages on specifics? Do they know the basics of what they're asking about?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Uses terms incorrectly or asks for definitions of foundational concepts that should be pre-assumed.
**Score 2:** Surface-level familiarity — correct terminology but can't apply it to the specific situation.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Working knowledge of the relevant fundamentals. Cannot go one level deeper under probing but doesn't make basic errors.
**Score 4:** Demonstrates working knowledge and can engage one level deeper when Priya probes.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Demonstrates a level of preparation that surprises Priya. Uses domain terminology precisely, connects concepts accurately, and can go two levels deeper when pressed.

---

### 3. Active Listening and Synthesis
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the user absorbs expert answers and builds on them — not just acknowledging but demonstrating comprehension by applying, rephrasing, or probing the specific content of what was said.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user say "got it" and move on? Do they reference the previous answer in their next question? Do they apply the answer to their specific case?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Says "got it" or "makes sense" without any signal of actual comprehension. Next question is unrelated to what was just explained.
**Score 2:** References the previous answer superficially — repeats a phrase from it without applying the underlying concept.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Demonstrates comprehension by accurately paraphrasing the previous answer before asking the next question.
**Score 4:** Explicitly applies what was just explained to their specific case and identifies an implication that changes their next question.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Integrates the previous answer so completely that their next question could only be formed by someone who deeply understood it. Priya can visibly see the thinking happening in real time.

---

### 4. Knowing What You Don't Know
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user is precise and honest about the limits of their understanding — naming exactly what they're unsure about and why that specific gap matters.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user bluff through knowledge gaps? Do they acknowledge uncertainty in vague terms ("I'm not sure about this part") or with precision ("I understand X but can't figure out why Y applies in the case where Z")?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Bluffs through obvious knowledge gaps with false confidence. OR asks for validation of decisions already made rather than genuine inquiry.
**Score 2:** Acknowledges uncertainty in vague terms without naming what specifically is uncertain or why it matters.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Names what they don't know precisely, but doesn't explain why that specific gap matters for their problem.
**Score 4:** Names the specific gap, explains why it matters for their decision, and asks a question that would directly address it.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Treats "I don't know" as a map — names the gap, the reason it matters, what they'd need to understand to fill it, and how that would change their approach.

---

### 5. Engagement with Complexity
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user can handle nuanced or conditional answers — probing the conditions and exceptions rather than collapsing complexity into a simple rule.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user try to reduce every conditional answer to a simple rule? Do they probe the conditions where the answer changes? Do they engage with the "it depends" rather than trying to get past it?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Tries to reduce every complex answer to a simple rule ("so basically always do X?"). Becomes lost when Priya gives a conditional answer.
**Score 2:** Accepts complexity without reducing it, but doesn't engage with the conditions — nods along without testing understanding.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Asks about one condition or exception from a complex answer.
**Score 4:** Probes multiple conditions and tests their application to their specific situation.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Treats conditionality as the most useful part of the answer. Asks "what has to be true for that answer to change?" and can reason about the answer.

---

### 6. Quality of Follow-Up Questions
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user's follow-up questions build directly on the previous answer — showing they absorbed the content, not just the conclusion.
**Things to Look For:** Could the follow-up question have been asked before hearing the previous answer? Does it reference something specific from the previous answer? Does it go one level deeper?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Follow-up questions are the same as the initial questions — preprepared rather than responsive to the expert's answer.
**Score 2:** Follow-up questions are related to the topic but don't build directly on the previous answer.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Follow-up questions clearly reference the previous answer and ask a natural extension of it.
**Score 4:** Follow-up questions reveal that the user absorbed the specific nuance in the previous answer — they reference the condition, exception, or implication that Priya emphasized.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Follow-up questions are so well-constructed that Priya says "good" before answering. They go one level deeper than the previous answer, identify a non-obvious implication, or challenge a condition that Priya assumed was settled.

---

### 7. Precision of Language
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user uses technical language precisely — not just to signal familiarity but to communicate accurately.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user use terms correctly? Do they use technical vocabulary to clarify or to obfuscate? When they're not sure of the right term, do they describe the concept rather than guessing at the word?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Uses technical vocabulary incorrectly in ways that reveal misunderstanding. Or uses a technical term to signal sophistication without applying it correctly.
**Score 2:** Uses terms roughly correctly at the surface level but with imprecision that would matter in a technical argument.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Uses relevant terms correctly and consistently. No significant misuse.
**Score 4:** Precise use of technical language that communicates accurately and makes Priya's job of answering easier.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Uses language so precisely that Priya can answer without needing to re-translate. When the user doesn't know the right term, they describe the concept clearly enough that the term becomes obvious.

---

### 8. Intellectual Honesty
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user admits when they were wrong, when an assumption they brought in was mistaken, or when Priya's answer changed their view — rather than defending an initially wrong position.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user update visibly when Priya corrects an assumption? Do they acknowledge when a question was poorly formed after receiving an answer? Do they resist updating?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Defends incorrect assumptions or incorrect framings even after Priya corrects them. Treats being corrected as losing rather than learning.
**Score 2:** Acknowledges corrections verbally ("you're right") without demonstrating that the update actually changed anything.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Updates their position when corrected and demonstrates the update through a modified follow-up question.
**Score 4:** Proactively acknowledges when their initial framing was wrong without being prompted.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Uses their mistakes as productive inputs — says "I assumed X, but hearing your answer, that assumption was wrong because Y, which means my original question should have been Z instead."

---

### 9. Respecting Expert Time
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user respects that this is a limited and valuable resource — asking questions efficiently, not repeating ground that was covered, and not asking questions that could have been googled.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user ask questions that waste Priya's time? Do they repeat questions that were already answered? Do they ask about things they should have researched beforehand?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Asks multiple questions that could be answered by reading a foundational resource. Repeats questions that were already answered.
**Score 2:** Occasionally wastes time with questions below the level of the consultation.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Consistently uses the consultation time on questions that genuinely require expert knowledge.
**Score 4:** Uses the consultation so efficiently that Priya never feels time is being wasted.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Uses time so well that Priya voluntarily extends the session. Every question is calibrated to what only a 15-year expert can answer.

---

### 10. Problem Definition Clarity
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user can articulate their actual problem precisely — not just the symptom or the area of interest, but the specific decision or constraint they are trying to resolve.
**Things to Look For:** Can the user state their problem in one or two sentences at any point? Can they distinguish between what they're building, what constraint they're facing, and what question they're asking?
**Score 1 — Weak:** The problem remains undefined throughout the consultation. Priya has to ask multiple clarifying questions before understanding what's actually being asked.
**Score 2:** Problem is defined by the end of the conversation but took significant time to surface.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Problem is defined at the start of the consultation with reasonable clarity.
**Score 4:** Problem is defined precisely at the start — Priya can immediately engage with it.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Problem definition is so clear and well-bounded that Priya can anticipate the follow-up questions before the user asks them.

---

### 11. Hypothesis Formation
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user arrives with a hypothesis or initial answer they're testing — rather than asking open-ended questions and waiting for the expert to think for them.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user state what they think the answer might be before asking? Do they frame their questions as tests of a hypothesis they've already formed?
**Score 1 — Weak:** All questions are open-ended. The user has no hypothesis and is expecting Priya to form one.
**Score 2:** Has a vague intuition but can't articulate it clearly enough to test it.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Has a hypothesis and states it before asking the question: "My assumption is X — is that right?"
**Score 4:** Has a well-formed hypothesis, states it clearly, and asks a question designed to test a specific condition.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Arrives with multiple hypotheses ranked by likelihood, and uses the consultation to systematically test them. Can articulate what evidence would falsify each one.

---

### 12. Synthesis Across Answers
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user synthesizes multiple answers from the consultation into a coherent updated understanding — not just collecting answers but integrating them.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user connect earlier answers to later questions? Do they demonstrate that their mental model of the problem has changed over the course of the conversation?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Each question is independent. No synthesis or integration across the conversation.
**Score 2:** References an earlier answer in a later question but doesn't show how the two connect.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Synthesizes two answers into a connected insight at some point in the conversation.
**Score 4:** Demonstrates updated understanding multiple times — explicitly connecting new answers to earlier ones.
**Score 5 — Strong:** At the end of the session, can articulate a coherent, integrated model that combines all the key insights from the consultation into a single framework they can act on.

---

### 13. Appropriate Pushback
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user challenges Priya's answers when they have a legitimate counter-consideration — rather than deferring to authority regardless of the merit of the answer.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user push back appropriately? Do they push back with evidence or just preference? Do they over-defer even when they have a valid counter-point?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Never pushes back regardless of the quality of the answer. Pure deference to authority.
**Score 2:** Pushes back based on preference or discomfort rather than evidence.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Pushes back with a specific consideration: "I thought [X] because of [Y] — does that change the answer?"
**Score 4:** Pushes back with evidence and a specific technical consideration that genuinely challenges Priya's framing.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Pushes back in a way that actually improves the conversation — identifies a condition Priya hadn't foregrounded and gets a richer answer as a result. Priya says "that's a fair challenge."

---

### 14. Practical Application Awareness
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user connects theoretical answers to their practical constraints — not just accepting an answer as abstract truth but asking what it means for their specific situation.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user always ask "so how does that apply to my situation?" Or do they treat every answer as general knowledge?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Accepts every answer as general knowledge without connecting it to their specific constraint.
**Score 2:** Occasionally connects an answer to their situation but inconsistently.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Consistently connects answers to their specific situation with appropriate follow-ups.
**Score 4:** Always asks how the answer changes their specific decision or approach.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Uses the answer to redefine the original problem — recognizes when the expert's answer reveals that the framing of the question was wrong, and updates the question accordingly.

---

### 15. Epistemic Calibration
**Weight:** 2
**Measures:** Whether the user accurately represents their confidence level — not overstating certainty or understating it in a way that wastes expert time re-explaining known material.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user say "I understand X" when they clearly don't? Do they say "I don't know anything about this" when they clearly do? Is their stated confidence level well-calibrated to their actual knowledge?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Significantly mis-states confidence — either claims knowledge they don't have or hides knowledge they do.
**Score 2:** Confidence is roughly calibrated but with one significant miss (over- or under-stated in a way that wastes time).
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Calibration is reasonably accurate. Priya can generally trust the user's self-report of their knowledge level.
**Score 4:** Precisely calibrated — Priya never has to recalibrate the conversation's starting point.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Uses confidence calibration as a tool — explicitly maps out "here is what I know with confidence, here is what I believe but haven't verified, here is what I genuinely don't know."

---

## Overall Score

Weighted average: sum(score × weight) / sum(weights). Scale 1–5.
Weights sum: 55.

**Calibration:** 4.0+ = a practitioner who earns Priya's genuine respect. 3.0-3.5 = competent but unremarkable. Below 2.5 = Priya would end the session early in a real consultation.

---

## Scoring Notes

Question Specificity (1), Domain Preparation (2), and Active Listening (3) are the three most diagnostic dimensions — they reveal whether the user actually did the preparation this kind of consultation requires. Intellectual Honesty (8) and Appropriate Pushback (13) are the character dimensions that separate genuine learners from approval-seekers.

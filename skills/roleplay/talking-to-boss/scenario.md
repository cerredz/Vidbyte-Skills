# Scenario: Talking to Your Boss

## Character Profile
**Name:** Jordan Rivera
**Role:** Engineering Manager, direct manager of the user — has been managing them for eight months
**Personality:** Task-oriented, briefly warm at the start of meetings, mentally elsewhere for the first minute, values brevity and action items over long context-setting
**Emotional State:** Mildly distracted — Jordan has back-to-back meetings today and glanced at their phone twice before this one started. Not hostile, but not fully present yet.

## Situation
Jordan and the user have a standing 1:1. Jordan has not prepared a specific agenda and is expecting the user to drive. There is an unresolved tension on the team: a project deadline was pushed back last week without clear communication to stakeholders, and Jordan knows it was handled messily but hasn't brought it up directly. The user may or may not be aware Jordan knows.

## Your Role
You are a software engineer on Jordan's team. You have something you want to raise — a concern, a request for feedback, an ask for a resource, or the deadline issue — it is up to you to choose your agenda.

## Character Goals
Jordan wants: (1) to know if there are blockers or risks on current work, (2) a concise update rather than a long narrative, and (3) for the conversation to surface something actionable so the meeting feels worthwhile. Jordan will gently but clearly redirect if the user over-explains context before getting to the point. If the deadline issue comes up, Jordan will engage with it directly but watch for whether the user takes ownership vs. deflects.

## Opening Line
> "Hey, good to connect — sorry, one second." *[brief pause]* "Okay, I'm here. What's on your mind this week?"

## Conversation Guidelines
- If the user gives a long context-setting preamble before the actual point, Jordan interjects: "Got it — and what's the ask or concern you're building toward?"
- If the user gets to a concrete point quickly and clearly, Jordan engages with it: "Yeah, that makes sense. What are you thinking in terms of next steps?"
- If the user raises the deadline issue with ownership ("I think we could have handled the stakeholder communication better, and here's what I'd do differently"), Jordan responds constructively and visibly appreciates the candor.
- If the user deflects blame on the deadline issue ("the requirements kept changing," "the other team didn't deliver"), Jordan stays neutral but probes: "What part of that could your team have handled better?"
- Escalation trigger: if the user repeatedly avoids getting to a point after two redirects, Jordan says "Let me be direct — what are you actually trying to raise here?"
- De-escalation trigger: if the user proposes a specific, reasonable next action with a clear owner and timeline, Jordan says "That works. Let's make that the plan."

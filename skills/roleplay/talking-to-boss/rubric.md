# Rubric: Talking to Your Boss

This rubric is calibrated for exceptionalism. A score of 5 should be rare. A score
of 3 means the employee met baseline expectations. Most people cluster at 2-3 across
most dimensions the first time they practice this scenario.

---

## Scoring Dimensions

### 1. Getting to the Point
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** How quickly and directly the user states their actual concern, ask, or agenda — without excessive context-setting that buries the point.
**Things to Look For:** How many turns pass before the actual concern is named? Does Jordan have to ask "what's the actual ask?" Does the user lead with the point or with background?
**Score 1 — Weak:** User spends 3+ turns building context before stating the actual ask. Jordan has to interrupt or explicitly ask "what's the point?" before the concern surfaces.
**Score 2:** User names their concern within 2 turns but only after Jordan prompts. The lead was buried.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User gets to the point within 2 turns without prompting, but with more preamble than the situation warrants.
**Score 4:** User states the core concern or ask in the first turn with minimal context-setting. The listener can immediately engage.
**Score 5 — Strong:** User leads with the actual ask in the first sentence — one sentence of context maximum — and then stops and waits. Jordan never has to redirect.

---

### 2. Ownership and Accountability
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the user takes responsibility for things within their control — especially if the deadline situation surfaces — rather than attributing problems to external factors.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user name their specific contribution to a problem? Do they use passive voice? Do they frame failure as external circumstance?
**Score 1 — Weak:** All problems are attributed to other people, unclear requirements, other teams, or external circumstances. No personal contribution named.
**Score 2:** Acknowledges a problem happened but uses "we" so broadly that no individual ownership is visible.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Names a shared contribution to a failure but doesn't specify what they personally did differently or would do differently.
**Score 4:** Names their specific contribution and describes a concrete behavioral change, but at a slightly high level.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Names exactly what they did (or failed to do), explains the downstream impact on others, and describes a specific behavioral change they made or will make — without excessive self-flagellation.

---

### 3. Proposed Next Action Quality
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the user comes with a concrete next step — not just a problem — and whether that proposed action has a named owner, a timeline, and a clear success condition.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user propose a solution or just a problem? If they propose a solution, does it have an owner? A deadline? A measurable outcome?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Raises a concern with no proposed path forward. Implicitly expects Jordan to solve it.
**Score 2:** Has a vague next step ("we should communicate better") with no owner, deadline, or measurable outcome.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Proposes a concrete next step with an owner but missing either a timeline or a success definition.
**Score 4:** Proposes a concrete next step with a named owner (including themselves), a realistic timeline, and a definition of success. Minor gap: the success definition is slightly vague.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Proposes a specific action, names the owner, states the deadline, and defines success in terms Jordan can verify without asking a follow-up question. Jordan can say yes or no immediately.

---

### 4. Reading the Room
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user notices and adapts to Jordan's cues — the mild distraction at the start, the preference for brevity, and the unstated tension around the deadline situation.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user adjust length when Jordan seems distracted? Do they proactively surface the uncomfortable topic? Do they match Jordan's level of directness?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Ignores all context cues — plows through a long monologue while Jordan is distracted, or acts surprised by direct challenge.
**Score 2:** Adapts to one cue (e.g., shortens answers after a redirect) but misses others (e.g., never surfaces the deadline topic).
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Picks up on Jordan's directness preference and adjusts pacing, but misses the meta-signal around the deadline situation.
**Score 4:** Adapts to multiple cues and either surfaces the uncomfortable topic proactively or addresses it confidently when it comes up.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Reads Jordan's state at the start (distracted, time-pressed) and adjusts the opening accordingly. Surfaces the uncomfortable topic on their own terms before Jordan has to. Matches Jordan's directness throughout.

---

### 5. Specificity of the Ask
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user's ask is specific enough to be actioned — not "I need more support" but "I need approval to deprioritize X in favor of Y by end of week."
**Things to Look For:** Can Jordan act on the ask without a follow-up question? Is the ask bounded in time and scope?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Ask is expressed as a general feeling or topic area with no specific action required of anyone.
**Score 2:** Ask is named but not bounded — no timeline, no scope, no clear deliverable.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Ask is specific and actionable but requires one follow-up question from Jordan to be executable.
**Score 4:** Ask is fully specified — Jordan knows exactly what is being requested and by when.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Ask is so precisely specified that Jordan can say yes or no in one sentence, with no clarifying questions needed.

---

### 6. Tone and Professionalism
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user maintains a professional, constructive tone — neither overly deferential nor inappropriately casual or blunt.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user complain rather than report? Do they hedge every statement to the point of sounding uncertain? Do they use the manager's time respectfully?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Vents, complains, or expresses frustration without constructive framing. OR is so over-deferential that the meeting becomes a performance of subordination rather than a working conversation.
**Score 2:** Professional but too passive — says "I was just wondering if maybe..." rather than "I'd like to propose..."
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Appropriately professional. Neither overly deferential nor inappropriately casual.
**Score 4:** Professional AND direct — makes clear asks, takes clear positions, while remaining respectful.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Models the kind of directness and respect that characterizes the best working relationships between managers and their reports. Treats Jordan as a partner, not a parent.

---

### 7. Handling Direct Pushback
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user stays constructive and solution-oriented when Jordan challenges them, redirects them, or presses on an uncomfortable topic.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user become defensive? Do they capitulate without reasoning? Do they hold their position with evidence?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Becomes defensive, apologetic to the point of being unhelpful, or completely capitulates without reasoning.
**Score 2:** Stays calm but becomes passive — gives shorter answers, waits for Jordan to lead, loses initiative.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Handles pushback without losing composure. Doesn't push back, but doesn't fall apart either.
**Score 4:** Handles pushback constructively and re-anchors to their core point. Minor gap: slightly passive in re-establishing their position.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Handles challenge directly — acknowledges the point, clarifies where they agree, maintains their position where they disagree with reasoning, and keeps the conversation moving toward a resolution.

---

### 8. Initiative and Preparation
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user came to the 1:1 with a prepared agenda or is improvising based on what Jordan says.
**Things to Look For:** Is there a clear structure to what they raise? Do they reference the current sprint, project status, or team goals? Do they seem to have thought about this before sitting down?
**Score 1 — Weak:** No apparent preparation — waits for Jordan to set the agenda.
**Score 2:** Has one topic to raise but nothing beyond it — the meeting ends as soon as that topic is covered.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Has a clear agenda item and handles it well. No evidence of preparation beyond the single topic.
**Score 4:** Came with an agenda, managed through it, and closes with a summary of next steps.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Came prepared with a tight agenda, managed through it efficiently, proactively surfaced a topic Jordan would have had to raise, and ended with a crisp summary of who owns what and by when.

---

### 9. Listening and Response Quality
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user actually absorbs what Jordan says and builds on it — not just waiting for their turn to speak.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user reference what Jordan just said? Do they ask follow-up questions that show comprehension? Do they adjust their approach based on Jordan's feedback?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Treats Jordan's responses as obstacles rather than information — continues with their prepared script regardless of what Jordan says.
**Score 2:** Hears Jordan but doesn't demonstrate comprehension — acknowledges without integrating.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Shows comprehension by incorporating Jordan's points into the next response.
**Score 4:** Actively builds on Jordan's contributions — uses them to refine the proposed solution or sharpen the ask.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Listens so well that the quality of their proposed solution improves visibly over the course of the conversation as they integrate Jordan's perspective.

---

### 10. Closing and Next-Steps Discipline
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** Whether the user closes the conversation with a clear summary of what was decided, who owns what, and by when — rather than letting the meeting end ambiguously.
**Things to Look For:** Is there a verbal summary of decisions and owners at the end? Does the user take responsibility for sending any follow-up? Are the next steps specific?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Meeting ends with no stated next steps. The user leaves without confirming what happens next.
**Score 2:** User acknowledges there should be follow-up but doesn't name it specifically.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** States one or two next steps but without owners or timelines.
**Score 4:** States all next steps with owners and timelines.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Closes with a crisp summary: "So to recap — I'll do X by Friday, you'll do Y by Thursday, and I'll send a Slack with both so we have it in writing." Jordan doesn't need to write anything down.

---

### 11. Framing of Problems
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user frames problems in a way that is useful to Jordan — factual, bounded, and impact-focused — rather than emotional, vague, or open-ended.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user describe what happened and what it affected? Or do they describe how they feel about it? Is the problem framed as a question for Jordan to solve or as a situation for them to collaborate on?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Problems are framed emotionally ("I feel like the team doesn't respect my input") rather than factually.
**Score 2:** Problems are framed factually but vaguely ("there are some communication issues").
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Problems are described factually and specifically, with impact named.
**Score 4:** Problems are framed as situations to collaborate on, with an initial proposed path forward already visible in the framing.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Problems are framed so well that Jordan can immediately see the decision they need to make — the situation, impact, options, and recommended path are all encoded in the framing.

---

### 12. Use of Evidence
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user supports their claims, concerns, and proposed solutions with evidence — not just assertion.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user cite specific incidents, data, or observations? Or are their claims general impressions?
**Score 1 — Weak:** All claims are assertions with no supporting evidence.
**Score 2:** One data point or example cited, but not enough to make the case.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Two or more relevant evidence points cited, making the case reasonably.
**Score 4:** Strong evidence base that makes the core claim well-supported. Minor gap: one assertion that would benefit from evidence.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Every significant claim is backed by a specific observable fact or data point. The case can stand on its own without Jordan extending the benefit of the doubt.

---

### 13. Recognizing Jordan's Implicit Signals
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user notices when Jordan signals disengagement, impatience, or redirection — and adapts before Jordan has to be explicit.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user pick up on Jordan saying "got it" (meaning move on) versus genuinely engaging? Do they adjust when Jordan asks a re-centering question? Do they over-explain after Jordan has signaled comprehension?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Continues long explanations after Jordan has signaled multiple times that they've heard enough.
**Score 2:** Picks up on explicit redirects but misses subtler signals.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Correctly interprets most of Jordan's signals. One instance of over-explaining after comprehension was signaled.
**Score 4:** Reads Jordan's signals accurately and adjusts without being explicitly asked.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Anticipates signal changes — adjusts pacing, depth, and direction before Jordan has to redirect. The conversation flows without friction.

---

### 14. Vulnerability Without Weakness
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user can share uncertainty, gaps, or struggles in a way that is honest and professional — rather than either hiding them or presenting them in a way that requires Jordan to manage their emotions.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user share only curated success stories, or do they also name what they're unsure about? If they share a struggle, do they still own the resolution path?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Only shares wins and confident takes — no honest acknowledgment of where they're struggling or uncertain.
**Score 2:** Shares a struggle but frames it in a way that requires Jordan to provide reassurance or emotional support.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Shares a struggle honestly and without requiring emotional management from Jordan. Doesn't propose a clear path forward.
**Score 4:** Shares a struggle honestly, names the impact, and proposes their current plan for addressing it.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Shares uncertainty and struggles in a way that is completely unencumbered — Jordan hears it as information, not as a request for help. The employee is clearly managing themselves.

---

### 15. Efficiency of the Interaction
**Weight:** 2
**Measures:** Whether the user respects Jordan's time — covering what matters without padding, without repetition, and without extending the meeting beyond its useful purpose.
**Things to Look For:** Does the user repeat themselves? Do they extend the conversation past the point where all agenda items are covered? Is the interaction tight?
**Score 1 — Weak:** Meeting is padded with repetition, rehashing already-covered ground, or extending past the productive point.
**Score 2:** Moderately efficient — some repetition or one significant tangent.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** Reasonably efficient. Covers the agenda without significant padding.
**Score 4:** Efficient and purposeful — covers what matters, skips what doesn't.
**Score 5 — Strong:** Maximally efficient. Jordan never feels like time is being wasted. The meeting ends when the work is done, not when the time slot runs out.

---

## Overall Score

Weighted average: sum(score × weight) / sum(weights). Scale 1–5.
Weights sum: 54.

**Calibration:** 4.0+ = genuinely impressive — most experienced professionals score 3.0-3.5. A 2.5 or below means the user left Jordan with more work than before the meeting.

---

## Scoring Notes

Dimensions 1 (Getting to the Point), 2 (Ownership), 3 (Next Action Quality), and 7 (Handling Pushback) are the primary signal dimensions. A user who is warm and likable but scores 2 or below on these four is a management burden regardless of their other strengths. Closing and Next-Steps Discipline (10) is systematically underweighted by most people in this scenario — the best candidates treat it as essential, not optional.

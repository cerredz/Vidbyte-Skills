# Rubric: Talking to Your Boss

## Scoring Dimensions

### Getting to the Point
**Weight:** 4
**Measures:** How quickly and directly the user arrives at their actual concern, ask, or agenda — without excessive context-setting that buries the lead.
**Score 1 — Weak:** User spends more than two full turns building context before stating what they actually want or need. Manager has to interrupt or explicitly ask "what's the ask?"
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User gets to the point within two turns but with more background than the situation requires. The core message is findable but takes effort.
**Score 5 — Strong:** User leads with the actual concern or ask in the first turn, then provides supporting context only if asked. Jordan never has to prompt them forward.

### Ownership and Accountability
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the user takes responsibility for things within their control — especially relevant if the deadline issue surfaces — rather than deflecting to external factors.
**Score 1 — Weak:** User consistently attributes problems to other people, unclear requirements, or circumstances outside their control without acknowledging any personal or team contribution.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User acknowledges something went wrong but frames it primarily as a shared or external problem rather than naming their own specific contribution.
**Score 5 — Strong:** User names what they specifically could have done differently, shows they understand the downstream impact on stakeholders, and frames it as a learning rather than a complaint.

### Proposed Next Action Quality
**Weight:** 5
**Measures:** Whether the user comes with a concrete next step — not just a problem — and whether that proposed action is specific enough to be actionable (has an owner, a timeline, and a clear outcome).
**Score 1 — Weak:** User raises a concern or problem and either has no next step in mind or proposes something vague ("we should probably communicate better going forward").
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User has a proposed next step but it is missing a key element: no owner named, no timeline, or the outcome is too vague to evaluate.
**Score 5 — Strong:** User proposes a next action with a named owner (including themselves), a realistic timeline, and a clear definition of what success looks like. Jordan can immediately say yes or no.

### Reading the Room
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user notices and adapts to Jordan's cues — distraction at the start, directness preference, and the unstated tension around the deadline issue.
**Score 1 — Weak:** User ignores context cues entirely — plows ahead with a long monologue while Jordan is clearly distracted, or acts surprised by direct challenges.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User picks up on some cues (e.g., adjusts length after Jordan's redirect) but misses others (e.g., never surfaces the deadline issue at all even when it's relevant).
**Score 5 — Strong:** User adjusts proactively — keeps early turns short while Jordan settles in, surfaces the sensitive topic directly rather than hoping it won't come up, and matches Jordan's preferred level of directness.

### Professionalism Under Pressure
**Weight:** 3
**Measures:** Whether the user stays constructive and solution-oriented when Jordan challenges them, redirects them, or pushes on an uncomfortable topic.
**Score 1 — Weak:** User becomes defensive, apologetic to the point of being unhelpful, or shuts down when challenged. Conversation stalls.
**Score 3 — Adequate:** User stays calm but becomes passive — gives short answers, waits for Jordan to lead, loses the initiative they started with.
**Score 5 — Strong:** User handles direct challenge or redirection without losing composure, re-anchors to their core point, and keeps the conversation moving toward an outcome.

## Overall Score
Weighted average: sum(score × weight) / sum(weights). Scale 1–5.
Weights sum: 20.

## Scoring Notes
Ownership and Proposed Next Action are equally the highest-weight dimensions. A user who is professionally smooth but consistently deflects blame or never proposes a concrete action should not score above 3.0 overall, regardless of how composed they appear.

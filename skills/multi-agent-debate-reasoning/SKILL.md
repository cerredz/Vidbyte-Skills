# /multi-agent-debate-reasoning — Multi-Agent Debate Meta-Reasoner

## Goal

Pair structured multi-agent debate with any Vidbyte reasoning strategy to produce answers that have survived adversarial collaboration. Three heterogeneous agents—the Optimizer, the Skeptic, and the Integrator—each independently apply the selected reasoning strategy to the user's problem, then engage in three structured debate rounds where they critique one another's positions, identify gaps, surface hidden assumptions, and revise toward convergence. The meta-skill records every position, critique, and revision transparently so the user can audit which claims survived scrutiny, which disagreements persisted, and where the consensus ultimately settled.

The result is reasoning that has been stress-tested from multiple angles before delivery. Rather than a single model producing a single answer with unknown blind spots, the debate architecture forces each claim to survive cross-examination from an agent with a deliberately different epistemic posture. Claims that survive the Optimizer's aggressive solution-finding, the Skeptic's systematic doubt, and the Integrator's demand for coherence are more robust than claims any single agent could produce alone. The trace preserves the full debate history—initial positions, critiques, revisions, and convergence points—so the user sees not just what the answer is but why it withstood adversarial pressure and where residual uncertainty remains.

## Intent

The purpose is to apply the multi-agent debate (MAD) research technique—formalized by Du et al. (2023) in "Improving Factuality and Reasoning through Multiagent Debate"—to stress-test Vidbyte strategy execution through structured adversarial collaboration. Single-agent reasoning, even when the agent follows a rigorous strategy, is vulnerable to blind spots: confirmation bias, missing counterarguments, unexamined assumptions, and premature convergence on the first plausible answer. By instantiating three heterogeneous agents with deliberately distinct cognitive roles and forcing them to critique one another's outputs across three structured rounds, the meta-skill surfaces weaknesses that any single agent would miss and produces answers with measurable robustness.

This meta-skill also operationalizes a key finding from the MAD literature: homogeneous agent ensembles that simply vote or average do not consistently outperform single agents, because shared model biases produce correlated errors that majority voting cannot correct. Heterogeneous agents—each assigned a genuinely distinct epistemic role—create the cognitive diversity necessary for debate to generate insight rather than amplify error. The Optimizer pushes for the strongest possible solution, the Skeptic hunts for flaws and alternative explanations, and the Integrator demands that the final output cohere into a consistent, actionable whole. This is not three agents agreeing on the same answer; it is three agents with different priorities forcing one another to defend, refine, and reconcile their positions until convergence emerges from friction.

## Background — What Is Multi-Agent Debate Reasoning

Multi-agent debate reasoning operationalizes the insight that structured disagreement produces better answers than solitary deliberation. Rooted in Minsky's Society of Mind principle—that intelligence emerges from the interaction of many simple, specialized agents rather than from a single monolithic reasoner—MAD instantiates multiple language model instances with distinct cognitive roles and has them debate a question through successive rounds of position-stating, critique, and revision. Du et al. (2023) demonstrated that multi-agent debate improves both mathematical reasoning accuracy (measured on GSM8K) and factual accuracy (measured on truthfulness benchmarks), with successive debate rounds producing increasing convergence toward correct answers. The key mechanism is that each agent sees the others' responses and must either defend its position against specific critiques or revise in light of superior arguments, creating a selection pressure toward claims that can survive adversarial scrutiny. Critically, Du et al. found that heterogeneous agent configurations—where agents have distinct roles, personas, or reasoning styles—outperform homogeneous configurations where agents are identical, because homogeneous agents share the same blind spots and their debate devolves into mutual reinforcement rather than genuine adversarial testing. The debate architecture records disagreements transparently rather than forcing false consensus, ensuring that unresolved tensions are surfaced to the user rather than papered over.

## Algorithm

1. **Detect Invocation:** Parse the user's prompt to identify which Vidbyte reasoning strategy domain is being invoked. Extract the core question, constraints (platform, duration, audience, evidence standards), and expected deliverable format. If no explicit strategy is named, flag this for the classification step. Confirm that the multi-agent-debate meta-skill is active and that all agent role definitions are loaded.

2. **Clarify Ambiguities:** Before proceeding, identify any missing information that would materially affect strategy execution or debate quality. Check for underspecified objectives, audience segments, success criteria, or evidence standards. If critical gaps exist, formulate exactly one clarifying question—never more—and pause for the user's response before continuing. Debate quality depends on clear problem specification; an ambiguous question produces unfocused debate that converges on the wrong target.

3. **Web Search for Skills if Not Installed:** Verify that the requested domain strategy skill is available in the current environment. If it is not installed, execute a targeted web search to locate the skill definition, install it, and confirm activation before proceeding to classification. The debate meta-skill is useless without the underlying strategy skill to apply.

4. **Classify the Request:** Map the user's intent to the correct domain bucket from the Reasoning Arsenal. If the request spans multiple domains, identify the primary domain and note secondary domains for cross-pollination during debate. If no domain clearly matches, default to the closest fit and annotate the classification as low-confidence. The strategy classification determines which reasoning move the agents will apply.

5. **Select Strategy from Arsenal:** Load the full strategy specification for the classified domain. Confirm that all required inputs are present. If optional inputs would improve output quality or debate depth, note them but do not block execution. Initialize the execution context with user parameters and define the debate format: number of rounds (default 3), convergence threshold (when positions are close enough to terminate early), and disagreement-handling protocol (how to characterize unresolved disputes).

6. **Define Three Heterogeneous Agents with Distinct Roles:** Instantiate the three agents with clearly differentiated epistemic postures. The **Optimizer** role is to produce the strongest, most complete application of the selected strategy—this agent prioritizes solution quality, comprehensiveness, and actionability, pushing for the best possible answer with maximal supporting reasoning. The **Skeptic** role is to find every weakness, gap, hidden assumption, alternative explanation, and failure mode in others' positions—this agent prioritizes critical scrutiny, demanding evidence for every claim and surfacing counterarguments that others missed. The **Integrator** role is to reconcile competing positions, identify where agents actually agree versus where they genuinely differ, synthesize partial agreements into a coherent whole, and characterize residual disagreements clearly—this agent prioritizes coherence, completeness, and honest uncertainty representation. Each agent must produce output that is recognizably distinct from the others; a Skeptic that agrees with everything and an Optimizer that doubts everything are role failures.

7. **Execute Three Structured Debate Rounds:** Run the debate through three rounds with full transparency. **Round 1 — Initial Positions:** Each agent independently applies the selected reasoning strategy to the user's problem and produces a complete initial position. These positions are shared with all agents. **Round 2 — Structured Critique:** Each agent reviews the other two positions and produces targeted critiques. The Optimizer critiques for missed opportunities, suboptimal solutions, and insufficient ambition. The Skeptic critiques for logical gaps, unsupported claims, hidden assumptions, and alternative explanations. The Integrator critiques for internal inconsistency, unclear tradeoffs, and unresolved tensions between competing claims. Critiques must reference specific claims from the target position; generic criticism is not allowed. **Round 3 — Revision and Convergence:** Each agent revises its position in light of the critiques received. Agents must explicitly acknowledge which critiques they accepted and incorporated, which they rejected and why, and where their revised position now converges with or diverges from other agents' positions. The Integrator produces a final convergence assessment: claims all three agents agree on (consensus zone), claims two of three agree on (partial consensus zone), and claims where disagreement persists with a clear characterization of the nature and significance of each disputed point.

8. **Write Trace with Full Debate History:** Produce a structured execution trace containing: the strategy domain and parameters used, the three agent role definitions with their distinct priorities, each agent's full Round 1 position, all Round 2 critiques with cross-references to the claims they target, each agent's Round 3 revision with accept/reject annotations for every critique, the Integrator's convergence assessment with consensus/partial-consensus/disagreement zones, and the final synthesized answer. Append this trace to the strategy output so the user can audit every claim's survival through adversarial testing. If convergence was not achieved on a material point, the trace must characterize the nature of the disagreement explicitly rather than pretending it does not exist.

## Reasoning Arsenal

**Causal and Diagnostic Domain:** Strategies that trace cause-and-effect relationships to explain why something happened or diagnose root causes. Five Whys drills down through successive "why" layers to find the root cause beneath symptoms, effective for single-cause failures where the causal chain is linear and discoverable through iterative questioning. Root Cause Analysis maps a full causal tree including direct causes, contributing factors, and systemic conditions, suited for complex failures where multiple causes interact and the surface symptom is misleading. Fishbone (Ishikawa) categorizes potential causes into branches—people, process, technology, environment, materials, and measurement—for brainstorming possible causes before committing to investigation, widely used in manufacturing and process improvement. Fault Tree builds a top-down Boolean tree of events connected by AND/OR gates leading to a failure, essential for reliability engineering and safety-critical systems where failure paths must be exhaustively mapped. Bowtie Risk maps causes on the left, the central hazard event, and consequences on the right with barriers in between, providing a complete risk picture for safety cases and barrier effectiveness assessment. Event Tree forward-chains from an initiating event through branching possible outcomes, useful for accident progression analysis and probabilistic risk assessment after a trigger event occurs. Bottleneck Analysis identifies the single constraint most limiting throughput or system performance, applying Goldratt's Theory of Constraints to focus improvement effort where it will produce the largest system-level gain. Correlation vs. Causation distinguishes spurious correlations from genuine causal relationships, essential for evaluating research claims, interpreting observational data, and avoiding the trap of assuming that things that move together share a cause. Regression Reasoning models the relationship between variables and quantifies effect sizes, bridging statistical association and causal inference through controlled comparison and coefficient interpretation. Dependency Mapping charts what components depend on what others, identifying critical paths and single points of failure for infrastructure analysis, project planning, and architectural risk assessment.

**Logical and Formal Domain:** Strategies that derive conclusions through formal logical structures with explicitly stated rules and premises. Deductive Reasoning derives conclusions from premises through logically necessary steps, appropriate when rules and definitions fully determine the answer and the problem is one of correct application rather than discovery. Inductive Reasoning generalizes from specific observations to broader patterns or principles, suited for pattern recognition and theory building from examples where the conclusion is probably true but not guaranteed. Abductive Reasoning generates competing explanations for observed phenomena and selects the best-supported one, the natural reasoning mode for diagnostic problems, mystery-solving, and any situation where the conclusion is not logically forced but represents the best available explanation. Syllogistic Reasoning tests categorical logic through major premise, minor premise, and conclusion chains, effective for formal logic problems and any domain where categorical classification and rule application are central. Propositional Logic evaluates truth values of compound statements using logical operators (AND, OR, NOT, IF-THEN), essential for Boolean reasoning, conditional logic, and formal verification of logical arguments. Predicate Logic extends propositional logic with quantifiers (for all, there exists) to reason about properties and relations, necessary for formal specification, relational reasoning, and quantified statements about domains. Modal Reasoning handles necessity, possibility, obligation, permission, and other modalities, critical for counterfactual thinking ("what must be," "what could be"), normative analysis of obligations, and possibility space exploration. Nonmonotonic Reasoning draws conclusions that can be retracted when new information arrives, matching real-world reasoning where information is incomplete and beliefs must be revisable without contradiction. Defeasible Reasoning builds arguments that hold by default but admit exceptions, the natural structure for legal reasoning, policy analysis, and any domain governed by rules with exceptions. Fuzzy Logic reasons with degrees of truth rather than binary true/false, handling graded concepts, partial membership, and approximate reasoning where crisp boundaries would misrepresent reality. Proof by Cases breaks a problem into exhaustive cases and proves each independently, suited for classification problems and conditional reasoning with multiple branches. Proof by Contradiction assumes the negation of what you want to prove and derives an impossibility, effective for existence proofs, impossibility results, and rigorous refutation of claims.

**Decision and Evaluation Domain:** Strategies that structure choices between options by surfacing tradeoffs, quantifying consequences, and applying explicit decision criteria. Decision Tree maps decisions, chance events, and outcomes as a branching structure with expected values at terminal nodes, the standard tool for sequential decisions under uncertainty and multi-stage choices. Cost-Benefit Analysis quantifies and compares all costs and benefits of each option in common units, essential for resource allocation, investment evaluation, and policy assessment where tradeoffs can be monetized. Expected Value weights each possible outcome by its probability to compute the average expected result, the foundational decision tool for risky choices, probabilistic payoffs, and financial decisions under uncertainty. Tradeoff Matrix scores options across weighted criteria to surface the best-balanced choice, ideal for multi-criteria decisions, vendor selection, and technology comparisons where no option dominates on every dimension. Satisficing finds the first option that meets all minimum thresholds rather than optimizing, the correct approach for time-constrained decisions and "good enough" problems where exhaustive search is impractical. Regret Minimization evaluates options by the maximum regret you would feel if you chose wrong, suited for irreversible decisions, high-stakes personal choices, and the "what will I wish I had done" frame for life decisions. Opportunity Cost evaluates what you give up by choosing each option, forcing explicit consideration of foregone alternatives in resource allocation, build-vs-buy, and time investment decisions. Utility Analysis models preferences as a utility function and maximizes expected utility, handling decisions involving risk preferences, subjective value, and non-linear payoffs where expected monetary value misrepresents preferences. Minimax chooses the option that minimizes your maximum possible loss, the principled approach for adversarial decisions, worst-case planning, and competitive strategy against an intelligent opponent. Values Tradeoff surfaces and weighs competing values when options optimize for different principles, essential for ethical decisions, mission tradeoffs, and value-laden choices where metrics alone cannot adjudicate. AB Testing designs and analyzes controlled experiments comparing two variants, the gold standard for product decisions, UI optimization, and any domain where empirical comparison is possible.

**Probabilistic and Forecasting Domain:** Strategies that reason under uncertainty by quantifying likelihoods, updating beliefs with evidence, and projecting possible futures. Bayesian Reasoning updates belief probabilities as new evidence arrives using Bayes' theorem, the normative framework for evidence-based belief revision, diagnostic reasoning, and learning from data. Probabilistic Reasoning assigns and propagates probabilities through structured analysis, essential for risk quantification, uncertainty propagation, and stochastic modeling of complex systems. Base Rate anchors probability estimates in the underlying base rate before adjusting for specifics, the critical technique for avoiding the base rate fallacy in diagnostic testing, screening decisions, and any probability judgment. Uncertainty Quantification explicitly bounds and characterizes uncertainty in estimates and predictions, necessary for scientific modeling, engineering estimates, and policy forecasting where precision claims must be defendable. Sensitivity Analysis identifies which uncertain inputs most affect the output, guiding data collection effort and revealing where model conclusions are fragile vs. robust. Scenario Planning develops multiple distinct, coherent futures and plans for each, the standard tool for long-range strategy under deep uncertainty where prediction is impossible. Cone of Plausibility maps the expanding range of possible futures from now outward, useful for horizon scanning, long-term strategic foresight, and mapping the possibility space before narrowing to specific scenarios. Reference Class Forecasting uses outcomes from similar past projects as the baseline prediction, the empirically validated debiasing technique for project estimation, cost/duration forecasts, and avoiding the planning fallacy. Outside View predicts by looking at the distribution of outcomes for similar cases rather than the details of the current case, counteracting overoptimism and anchoring in project planning and investment decisions. What-If Analysis systematically varies assumptions and observes how outcomes change, valuable for stress testing, assumption exploration, and contingency planning against a range of possible conditions. Horizon Scanning identifies emerging trends, weak signals, and potential disruptions before they become obvious, the core activity of strategic foresight and early warning intelligence. Indicators and Signposts define observable metrics that would signal a scenario is unfolding, enabling monitoring programs, adaptive strategy, and trigger-based decision-making.

**Creative and Lateral Domain:** Strategies that generate novel solutions by breaking conventional thought patterns, introducing new perspectives, or systematically exploring possibility spaces. First Principles deconstructs a problem to its most fundamental truths and rebuilds from there, the approach for breaking out of conventional thinking, radical redesign, and problems where existing solutions are anchored to obsolete assumptions. Lateral Thinking approaches the problem from an unexpected angle to bypass standard thought patterns, effective for creative block, "impossible" problems, and situations where incremental improvement is insufficient. Reframing changes how the problem is defined to unlock new solution spaces, critical when a team is stuck because they have framed the problem in a way that excludes the best solutions. Constraint Removal imagines removing each constraint and explores what becomes possible, useful for innovation, surfacing hidden assumptions, and distinguishing between real constraints and habits. Provocation makes deliberately provocative statements to disrupt fixed thinking patterns, a technique for creative breakthroughs and challenging orthodoxies that have become invisible through familiarity. Reverse Brainstorming brainstorms how to cause the problem, then reverses the ideas into solutions, valuable for seeing the problem from the opposite direction and generating solutions to problems that resist direct attack. Random Stimulus introduces a random word or concept to trigger unexpected associations, suited for creative block when the mind needs an external perturbation to escape its current attractor. SCAMPER applies seven creative operations—Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse—systematically transforming existing solutions into new ones for product innovation and process redesign. TRIZ applies 40 inventive principles derived from global patent analysis to resolve contradictions, the most systematic engineering innovation methodology for technical problem solving and contradiction resolution. Synectics uses analogies and metaphors to make the strange familiar and the familiar strange, enabling creative concept development and making unfamiliar domains accessible through structured comparison. Biomimicry seeks solutions by studying how nature has solved analogous problems, a rich source of innovation for design, engineering, and sustainability challenges. Morphological Analysis decomposes the problem into dimensions and combines variations systematically, useful for design space exploration, feature combination, and structured ideation across multiple independent dimensions. Design Thinking cycles through empathize, define, ideate, prototype, and test in a human-centered problem-solving process, the dominant methodology for product design, user experience, and service design. Double Diamond diverges to explore broadly, converges to define, diverges again to develop, and converges to deliver, providing a meta-structure for design processes that balances exploration with refinement.

**Adversarial and Critical Domain:** Strategies that stress-test ideas, arguments, plans, and claims by simulating opposition, searching for weaknesses, and demanding evidence. Red Team simulates an adversary trying to defeat your plan or argument, essential for security analysis, plan stress-testing, and vulnerability assessment before deployment. Devil's Advocacy argues the strongest possible case against your position, useful for testing conviction strength, surfacing hidden weaknesses, and pressure-testing arguments before facing real opposition. Steelman constructs the strongest possible version of the opposing argument rather than attacking a weak caricature, the intellectually honest approach to understanding opponents and strengthening your own position through genuine engagement with the best counterarguments. Premortem imagines the project has already failed and works backward to identify causes, a powerful technique for project planning and risk identification that bypasses optimistic bias by starting from failure. Postmortem analyzes a completed project or event to extract lessons learned, essential for after-action review, incident analysis, and systematic learning from both successes and failures. Dialectical Reasoning moves through thesis, antithesis, and synthesis to reach deeper understanding, the classical method for complex debates where neither side is entirely right and the truth emerges from their interaction. Argument Mapping visualizes the structure of an argument—claims, evidence, rebuttals, and connections—enabling understanding of complex arguments, debate preparation, and logic checking through spatial representation. Analysis of Competing Hypotheses lists all plausible hypotheses, evaluates evidence for and against each, and selects the best-supported, the intelligence community's standard method for diagnostic reasoning and multiple-hypothesis problems. Key Assumptions Check lists every assumption underlying a conclusion and tests each for validity, sensitivity, and supporting evidence, surfacing the hidden architecture that would collapse the argument if any pillar fails. Null Hypothesis tests whether observed patterns could be explained by chance alone, the foundation of statistical inference, experimental interpretation, and any claim of effect or difference. Deception Detection analyzes information for indicators of deception, misrepresentation, or manipulation, applicable to fraud investigation, source evaluation, and credibility assessment of claims. Error Analysis systematically identifies, classifies, and traces the sources of errors, essential for debugging, quality improvement, and learning from mistakes through structured classification. OODA Red Team applies Observe-Orient-Decide-Act from an adversarial perspective, enabling competitive strategy analysis, security red-teaming, and rapid adversarial decision-making cycles.

**Systems Thinking Domain:** Strategies that analyze interconnections, feedback loops, emergent behaviors, and leverage points in complex adaptive systems. Systems Thinking maps interconnections, feedback loops, stocks, flows, and emergent behaviors, the foundational approach for understanding why complex systems resist change and produce unintended consequences. Causal Loop diagrams reinforcing and balancing feedback loops to reveal why systems behave as they do over time, essential for understanding policy resistance, growth dynamics, and unintended consequences of interventions. Iceberg Model moves from visible events to underlying patterns to systemic structures to mental models, finding the deepest leverage points for system change by descending through levels of explanation. Feedback Loop identifies and analyzes reinforcing loops that amplify change and balancing loops that stabilize systems, critical for understanding growth dynamics, market cycles, and ecological stability. Stock and Flow models accumulations and the rates that change them, providing the quantitative backbone of system dynamics for resource dynamics, population models, and inventory analysis. Leverage Points identifies where small changes can produce disproportionately large system-level effects, Donella Meadows' hierarchy for intervention design and maximum-impact strategic decisions. Nth-Order Effects traces consequences beyond first-order to surface hidden downstream impacts, essential for policy analysis, intervention planning, and the "and then what" reasoning that prevents solving one problem by creating a worse one. Second-Order Effects focuses specifically on the often-overlooked secondary consequences of actions, a subset of nth-order analysis useful for decision impact assessment and unintended consequence mapping. Theory of Constraints identifies the system's single bottleneck and subordinates everything else to maximizing flow through it, the operations management framework for throughput optimization in production, project, and service systems. Constraint Satisfaction defines constraints and finds solutions that satisfy all of them, useful for scheduling, resource allocation, and configuration problems where feasibility is the primary concern.

**Structured Analytic Domain:** Strategies that apply formal research and analysis methodologies to evaluate evidence, test hypotheses, and produce auditable conclusions. Scientific Method cycles through observation, question, hypothesis, experiment, analysis, and conclusion, the gold standard for empirical questions where controlled investigation is possible. Hypothesis Testing formulates testable hypotheses and designs experiments or observations to evaluate them against evidence, essential for empirical validation, research design, and claim verification. Experimental Design creates controlled experiments with proper randomization, treatment/control groups, and measurement protocols to establish causality, the foundation of evidence-based decision-making. Quasi-Experimental designs approximate experiments when randomization is not possible, necessary for policy evaluation, natural experiments, and observational causal inference in real-world settings. Randomized Control Trial implements gold-standard experiments with random assignment to treatment and control conditions, the strongest design for medical research, program evaluation, and rigorous causal testing. Evidence Triangulation cross-checks findings across multiple independent sources and methods, increasing confidence through convergent validity and identifying where single-source biases may have distorted conclusions. Data Quality Audit assesses data for completeness, accuracy, consistency, and reliability before analysis, preventing garbage-in-garbage-out failures in data-driven decisions and analytics pipelines. MECE Decomposition breaks a problem into Mutually Exclusive and Collectively Exhaustive parts, the consulting industry standard for problem structuring that ensures no overlap and no gaps. Issue Tree decomposes a complex question into a hierarchy of sub-questions, enabling systematic problem decomposition and research planning through progressive refinement. Minto Pyramid structures communication with the conclusion first, supported by grouped arguments that are themselves supported by data, the standard for business communication and executive summaries. Metacognitive Audit examines your own thinking process for biases, gaps, and unwarranted confidence, a self-assessment technique for improving decision quality through systematic introspection.

**Strategic and Business Domain:** Strategies that analyze competitive positioning, market dynamics, organizational choices, and multi-stakeholder decisions in business and policy contexts. SWOT Analysis evaluates Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats, the most widely used strategic planning framework for situational awareness and competitive positioning assessment. PESTLE analyzes Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental macro-factors, essential for market analysis, environmental scanning, and understanding the external context shaping strategic choices. Porter's Five Forces analyzes competitive intensity through supplier power, buyer power, rivalry, threat of substitutes, and threat of new entrants, the foundational framework for industry analysis and market entry decisions. Stakeholder Analysis maps who is affected by a decision, their interests, influence, and what they need, critical for project planning, change management, and policy design where buy-in determines success. Game Theory models strategic interactions where each player's outcome depends on others' choices, essential for competitive strategy, negotiation, auction design, and cooperation problems involving strategic interdependence. Incentive Analysis maps what each actor is rewarded for and what behavior the incentive structure actually produces, revealing the gap between intended and actual incentives in organization design and policy. Linchpin Analysis identifies the single assumption or condition everything else depends on, surfacing the critical vulnerability whose failure would cascade through the entire plan or argument. Policy Analysis evaluates policy options against criteria of effectiveness, efficiency, equity, and feasibility, the structured approach to policy design, regulation analysis, and program evaluation. OODA Loop cycles through Observe, Orient, Decide, Act for rapid situational decision-making, Boyd's framework for competitive dynamics and fast-moving tactical environments. Alternative Futures develops multiple coherent, divergent scenarios to inform strategy, enabling robust planning against a range of possible futures rather than optimization for a single prediction. Fairness Analysis evaluates outcomes across groups for disparate impact, equity, and procedural fairness, essential for algorithm auditing, policy fairness assessment, and distributional analysis. Ethical Matrix evaluates decisions through multiple ethical lenses—consequentialist, deontological, virtue ethics—providing structured moral reasoning for values-based decisions where single-principle analysis would be incomplete.

**Temporal and Historical Domain:** Strategies that reason about time, sequences, historical patterns, and the transfer of structure across domains. Temporal Reasoning handles sequences, durations, deadlines, and temporal constraints, necessary for scheduling, process timeline analysis, and any domain where ordering and timing are central. Historical Reasoning analyzes past events to identify patterns, causes, and lessons, enabling understanding of historical trajectories, learning from precedent, and tracing the genealogy of current ideas and institutions. Backward Chaining starts from the goal and works backward to identify necessary preconditions, the natural planning mode for goal-directed reasoning and proving theorems through reverse inference. **Specialized and Cross-Domain Strategies:** Strategies that combine multiple perspectives, methodological approaches, or domain-specific reasoning modes. ## Success Criteria

- Three genuinely distinct agent roles are instantiated—the Optimizer produces solution-focused output, the Skeptic produces critique-focused output, and the Integrator produces synthesis-focused output, with each agent's output recognizably reflecting its assigned epistemic posture.
- All three debate rounds are produced in full: Round 1 initial positions from each agent, Round 2 critiques from each agent targeting specific claims in the other positions, and Round 3 revisions with explicit accept/reject annotations for every critique received.
- The Integrator's final convergence assessment clearly characterizes three zones: claims all agents agree on (consensus), claims two of three agree on (partial consensus), and claims where disagreement persists with the nature and significance of each dispute.
- Claims that survive the full debate cycle are demonstrably more robust than any single agent's initial position, because every surviving claim has been explicitly critiqued and defended.
- The selected strategy domain is correctly classified before debate begins, and the strategy's core move is visibly applied by each agent in its reasoning.
- The debate trace records every position, critique, revision, and convergence point transparently, enabling a human reviewer to audit which claims survived scrutiny and which were revised or abandoned.
- Disagreements that persist after Round 3 are characterized honestly—their nature, significance, and the underlying reason for non-convergence are explained rather than papered over with false consensus.
- No agent role collapses into another; the Skeptic does not become a second Optimizer, the Integrator does not become a tie-breaking vote, and the Optimizer does not defer to the Skeptic's doubts without justification.
- The debate format adapts to the strategy's knowledge intensity—fact-heavy strategies produce more evidence-focused critiques, while creative strategies produce more possibility-expanding critiques.
- Clarification questions are limited to exactly one when ambiguities exist, and no execution proceeds without resolution of blocking gaps.
- If the required domain strategy skill is not installed, it is located via web search, installed, and verified before debate begins.
- The final answer presents the converged consensus position while explicitly flagging points where credible disagreement remains, rather than pretending unanimous agreement.
- Every agent's output includes an explicit confidence level, and the debate trace shows how confidence evolved across rounds.
- The execution trace is structured and self-contained, enabling downstream audit without requiring access to the original conversation context.
- The debate completes within the defined round limit (default 3) unless convergence is achieved earlier, in which case early termination is documented with the convergence threshold that was met.

## Things Not to Do

- Do not use identical or near-identical agent roles—if the Optimizer, Skeptic, and Integrator produce outputs that are substantively interchangeable, the debate has failed and must be rerun with stronger role differentiation.
- Do not force false consensus by having agents agree when genuine disagreement exists—the Skeptic must surface real weaknesses, not perform agreement, and unresolved disputes must be characterized transparently.
- Do not skip debate rounds—even if the initial positions appear to agree, Round 2 critique and Round 3 revision must be executed, because surface agreement often hides latent disagreements that critique would expose.
- Do not select prompt skills, learning skills, or session-management skills as the underlying strategy—only genuine reasoning trace strategies from the Arsenal may be paired with this meta-skill.
- Do not allow the Skeptic to critique without specificity—every critique must reference a specific claim from a target agent's position, and generic skepticism ("this could be wrong") is not a valid critique.
- Do not present the Integrator's synthesis as a simple majority vote—the Integrator must reconcile positions by identifying where agents actually agree on substance vs. where they use different language for the same insight, and where genuine differences remain.
- Do not execute the debate without first verifying that the required domain strategy skill is active and all mandatory inputs are present.
- Do not issue multiple clarifying questions in a single turn—exactly one, targeted at the most blocking ambiguity, then wait.

## Input

A natural-language prompt describing the Vidbyte strategy task, including the desired output format, target platform(s), content objective, audience profile, and any constraints (duration, tone, budget, brand guidelines). The prompt may name a specific strategy domain from the Arsenal or leave domain classification to the meta-skill. The multi-agent debate wrapper operates automatically once the underlying strategy is identified—no additional invocation syntax is required beyond the standard Vidbyte strategy prompt. Optional: explicit preference for debate intensity (more rounds, stricter convergence threshold) or agent role customization, though the default three-agent heterogeneous configuration is recommended for most tasks.
